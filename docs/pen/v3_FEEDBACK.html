Target Evaluation Feedback

The target environment evaluates each rollout from its exact simulation state: the wrist and object states it recorded at every control step, the contact flags of every control interval, the robot's joint log, and the finger actuation record. Nothing in the evaluation is estimated from cameras or sensors. Official success criteria are in SUCCESS.md. Feedback exists only after the rollout; the deployed policy receives the observation of CONTRACT.md and nothing else.

1. Official Evaluation

verifier/report.json (schema r2s2r-verifier/1, verifier gv-1) has the same top-level keys for every task:

FieldDescription

criteriaid, rubric and status of the success criteria the report was scored with: status provisional, approved, or undefined (SUCCESS.md)
statusevaluated, criteria_undefined, or not_evaluable (rule below)
successtrue or false; null unless status is evaluated
progressTask progress 0–1, the score of SUCCESS.md; null unless status is evaluated and the score could be computed (note says when it could not)
milestonesPer milestone name, stage, label, credit, reached, time (s since step 0; null if not reached). A stage with one milestone names it after the stage; otherwise <stage>_<i>. [] if no criteria
stagesPer stage name, weight, progress (0–1 within the stage), score. [] if no criteria

measurementsSummary quantities of the task's criteria (exact values; the keys of your task: TASK_CONFIG.md section 4; empty while no criteria are defined)
validityrun_valid; policy_runtime (pass, reason: the policy error, if any); physical (the target's own run checks R0_reference_config, R1_physics, R2_initial_state, R4_teleport, R9_integrity, each true/false); hardware (section 2); actions (pass, status); steps_executed, expected_steps, state_rows

noteA remark on the status (for example why nothing was scored), else null

Status rule:

  • evaluated: success criteria exist for the task. success is true only if the criteria are fully met (progress 1.0 and the final condition of SUCCESS.md), the hardware is compliant, the policy ran without failure and the run is valid; otherwise false. A policy failure or a hardware violation does not change the status: the record up to the failure is scored and success is false. A record too short for the final-window conditions of the criteria (a policy failure within the first 1.0 s, or before any step) is evaluated with success false and progress null: a score the criteria could not compute is never reported as a number; note says why. Such a submission is not ranked (history: rankable false, unranked_reason progress_not_computed).
  • criteria_undefined: no success criteria are defined for the task (criteria.status undefined). success and progress are null, milestones and stages are empty, measurements is empty; validity is still reported. Nothing is scored.
  • not_evaluable: the record itself is unusable (a target-side problem, explained in note); such a submission is not charged.

Exact values. Every value is computed from the target's simulation state; there are no measurement margins, uncertainty intervals or unknown outcomes. The evaluation reports what happened, not why: a stage that scored 0 is not explained, and a loss of retention gives its time, not the condition that ended it. Diagnosing causes from the state record, videos, frames and logs is your task.

2. Hardware and Runtime

validity.hardware applies hardware/SPECS.md section 7 to the finger joints over the whole policy phase, from the target's exact joint state and applied torque at the controller rate: status (compliant or violation), spec_version, mode, checks (position, velocity, effort, actions: each with its status, the worst joint and time, the maximum excess, the margin), violations, joints (the joints involved in a violation), max_ratios (velocity, effort relative to the limits; position_excess_rad), steps. The wrist has no rule beyond its controller; its tracking and saturation are visible in the state record and the rollout log, never a violation. validity.policy_runtime reports a policy failure with the error text; validity.physical lists the target's own run checks (reference configuration, finite state, initial state at rest, no position jumps, record integrity).

3. State Record

state/trajectory.npz is the ground-truth state of the rollout at the control rate, described field by field in state/schema.json (schema objstate-1: every field's shape, dtype, unit, frame, description; the conventions; rows, control_steps_executed, objects, parts, goals).

Rows and time. Row k (0 ≤ k ≤ N) is exactly the state the policy observed at control step k (the same instant and values as obs of CONTRACT.md); row N is the state after the last command, so a complete rollout has N + 1 rows for N steps (TASK_CONFIG.md section 2). After a policy failure at step k the file has rows 0..k. t = 0.002 · step s with t = 0 at control step 0, the same time base as logs/trial_0.npz (row k = step k, N rows) and video/frames.json. The contact flags of row k cover the control interval ending at the row (row 0: its own instant). Quaternions are (w, x, y, z), unit norm, sign-continuous as in CONTRACT.md.

R is the number of rows, O the number of objects and P the number of parts of your task. A task adds its own fields to the state record (per-part states, configuration indices, the goal block); they are listed with their shapes in TASK_CONFIG.md section 4 and described in state/schema.json.

The contact flags exist only in this feedback file; they are never part of the policy observation. The rows are the same values the policy saw, so the state record and your own simulator's record of the same action sequence can be compared row by row (TOOLS.md section 2).

4. Rollout Log

logs/trial_0.npz (N rows for a complete rollout; row k = control step k, up to the failure after a policy failure): joint_names (20,) (the joint names of your task's hand); the measured q, qd (N, 20) the policy received (with encoder noise); the finger commands q_target, qd_target (N, 20) as sent (None recorded as zeros); the wrist commands as sent, wrist_position_command (N, 3), wrist_orientation_command (N, 4), wrist_linear_velocity_command, wrist_angular_velocity_command (N, 3) (None recorded as zeros); the executed wrist targets after the command limits, wrist_position_target, wrist_orientation_target, wrist_linear_velocity_target, wrist_angular_velocity_target; and wrist_command_limited (N, 5) bool, one flag per limit in the order translation_rate, workspace, rotation_rate, linear_velocity, angular_velocity (true where the limit changed the command at that step). The executed targets are what the wrist controller tracked (hardware/SPECS.md section 4); the wrist state it reached is in the state record.

5. Videos and Cameras

The three videos are renderings of the target rollout: the recorded ground-truth trajectory of the hand (at its floating pose) and of the objects, drawn with the published camera model. They show the same data as state/trajectory.npz; no camera or sensor pipeline is part of the evaluation.

Three fixed, synchronized views top, left and right (global shutter, 848 × 480, pinhole without distortion); their placement for your task is in TASK_CONFIG.md section 3.

  • RGB video: H.264, real time, covering the whole rollout (up to the failure after a policy failure); the frame rate and the number of frames per view of a complete rollout are in TASK_CONFIG.md section 2.
  • Calibration: calibration.json: rig_version, layout, per view width, height, K, distortion, T_world_cam, T_cam_world, position_m, purpose; world frame as in hardware/SPECS.md; OpenCV camera frame, pixel (0, 0) at the centre of the top-left pixel; depth model; conventions.
  • Timestamps: video/frames.json gives each frame's time frame_t (s since control step 0) and control_step = round(frame_t × 500), the same for all views; video/video.json gives the fps, frame count, duration and the files.
  • Frames on demand: frames sNN VIEW TIMES [depth] (REMOTE_API.md section 6) renders the same trajectory at any instant, as RGB PNG and optionally 16-bit depth PNG in 0.1 mm units (0 = no return), noise σ = 1 mm × (z / 0.5 m)². Depth is available only through this query.

6. Standard Feedback

Evaluation evidence produced and stored automatically after every rollout (after a policy failure: up to the failure), never changed afterwards. It is not pushed to you; retrieve it with fetch sNN (REMOTE_API.md section 4).

8. Research Use

You may use the state record, videos, frames, logs and official results to diagnose failures and improve your simulation and policy. Your own diagnostic metrics do not replace the official evaluation.
