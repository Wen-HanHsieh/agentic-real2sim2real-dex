Criteria text

Simulation verification criteria v1

These are this experiment's own criteria, not an official evaluation. Defined before policy training or evaluation. Reference: reconstruction r0005 v1, research/recon_delivered, demo frames 0–394 with t=k/30 seconds, TASK.md "Solve the 2×2×2 Rubik's cube with one hand" and "especially the wrist pose".

Task understanding and ordered plan: establish a left-hand grasp; retain the scrambled cube against gravity; stabilize one layer while turning the other; change contacts/regrasp as needed; reach a solved configuration; retain the solved cube. The video also shows pickup (roughly frames67–84) and release (338–358). In this first curriculum, episodes start already held (reference frame100), so pickup and final release are outside the success claim. Hidden stickers and the demonstrated move sequence are unresolved; no claim of reproducing that exact scramble is possible.

State definitions and fixed protocol

The simulator must contain all eight physical cubies and passive legal-turn mechanics. World z is up. The cube center is the mean cubie center; puzzle orientation is each cubie's orientation relative to cubie0, as in object/SPECS.md. Solved uses the supplied rotations24 table (identity index23). A 12-second episode is measured after any explicitly recorded grasp initialization; initialization is not allowed to solve or weld the cube. All initial puzzles must be legal, aligned and unsolved. No cube motor, grasp constraint, object teleportation, external support other than the floor, or execution-time search/controller deciding moves is allowed. Final actions must come from the closed-loop RL-trained network; an explicitly recorded retargeted trajectory with learned residuals is permitted by LEARNING.md.

Fixed evaluation set: 32 deterministic episode seeds 20261102 through20261133, same set for every iteration. Episodes0–15 use one random quarter turn;16–27 use two;28–31 use four, rejecting solved results and adjacent same-axis moves. Use the three fixed-corner generators (layers opposite cubie0, axes -X,-Y,+Z), with equiprobable turn signs; these describe legal cube moves, not policy instructions. Evaluate each tier separately as well as overall. Initial position perturbation is independent uniform ±3mm per axis, global orientation perturbation independent uniform ±5deg rotation-vector components, cube mass uniform[.057,.063]kg, sliding friction multiplier uniform[.8,1.2]. Exact sampled values must be saved. These are explicitly assumed local robustness ranges, not measured variation or evidence of general scramble solving.

Ordered measurable criteria

  1. Nontrivial start and left-hand support. At t=0 at least one cubie's relative orientation is >60deg from identity, all cubies are within1deg of a legal aligned configuration. Establish at least two distinct left-hand-link contacts with the cube within0.20s. The 60deg threshold excludes near-solved trivial starts; quarter turns give90deg. The 0.20s contact-settling allowance is an assumption for a held initial state, not time taken from a video pickup.
  2. Retention. Until completion, cube center z>0.060m, cube-center to palm-frame distance<0.150m, zero cube–floor contacts, and >=2 distinct hand contact links on >=90% of sampled controller ticks after0.20s. Thumb and an opposing finger must have contact points subtending >=60deg about the cube center on >=60% of those ticks. This implements the observed cupped one-hand support in frames85–337. Height leaves a50mm cube clear of the floor;150mm is a generous hand-scale reach bound. The contact percentages permit brief regrasp transitions and are assumptions. No other hand is present.
  3. Legal manipulation. Complete at least one physical quarter-turn transition (relative layer angle crosses a90deg alignment and settles within5deg); maximum cubie rotation deviation from the nearest allowed single-layer mechanism configuration is <=15deg at every physics tick. The break threshold is supplied by object/SPECS.md. The 5deg alignment tolerance is an assumed practical subset of the20deg detent basin. Simply rotating the whole cube cannot count as a layer turn.
  4. Solve and retain. By12s, all eight nearest relative-rotation indices are23 AND each relative quaternion is within5deg of identity, continuously for0.50s while retention remains satisfied. This encodes "solve", and prevents transient near-alignment from passing. The0.50s hold is an explicit assumption that the observed final uniform faces correspond to a stable solve. This test alone cannot certify every mechanism detail, so criterion3 is also required.
  5. Hardware compliance throughout. At every1ms physics tick: every joint remains within URDF[lower-.02,upper+.02], absolute velocity <=specified maximum, absolute applied motor torque <=effort limit; finite20-vector position targets clipped to URDF limits, with missing velocity target treated as zero. Use hardware/hardware_spec_left.json and hardware/SPECS.md; floating-wrist force/torque, gains, gravity compensation and target-speed limits must also be implemented and logged. Saturation is reported separately from a violation. A hardware violation fails the episode even if the cube solves.
  6. Wrist resemblance throughout. Use the left-hand reconstructed wrist/palm axes from wrist and MCP joints, retargeted to the robot mount axes (+X toward thumb, +Y dorsal, -Z fingers). Save the exact fixed transform, scale and interpolation in the simulation record before training. Compare the actual robot wrist at each time to the fixed retargeted reference from frames100–337, followed by a final pose hold. Compute SO(3) geodesic rotation RMS <=35deg and position RMS <=0.060m over the episode up to completion/termination. Do not fit an alignment to policy rollouts. Tolerances accommodate the documented monocular uncertainty, differing anatomy and contact-driven corrections; they are assumptions, not measured human variance. Report both metrics even on failed episodes and report termination time so short failures cannot masquerade as successful tracking.

Success requires criteria1–4 in order and criteria5–6 throughout. A progress report gives support fraction, legal turns, smallest orientation error, longest solved hold, hardware maxima, wrist errors and termination reason. Report successes/32 plus tier counts, and retain every failed episode's per-criterion measurements. If no policy succeeds, report failure; reward improvement is not task success.

Ambiguities and limits

The video cube's metric size is unknown (we assigned the task50mm edge); actual scramble, hidden cubie identities and turns are not reconstructed. The reference has uncertain contact opposition and some invalid hand frames; interpolate only for the explicitly inferred control reference and retain those gaps in the original reconstruction. A solved initial state is never counted as success. Perfect simulated object state may be observed by the policy, but hand contact/force sensing must not be invented; any privileged observations are a simulation-only limitation. Physics parameters not supplied in SPECS are assumptions and require model tests. These criteria make no real-robot or broad random-scramble claim.
