Criteria text

Agent verification criteria v1 — D2S-CUBE-7

These are my own simulation criteria, not an official task score. Written after the required reconstruction hour, before constructing the robot training simulation or evaluating a policy. Reference: reconstruction version 2, record r0007, research/recon_v2.

Task and move interface

A left Wuji Hand 2.2 with one common chosen starting posture receives a cube in the palm, then one requested move. I will evaluate six moves: U+, U− (the initially upward face), F+, F− (the initially fingertip-facing reachable face), and X+, X− (a whole-cube quarter roll about the initial cube axis most nearly parallel to the hand's lateral/thumb-side axis). A plus turn is a positive right-handed 90° rotation about the selected outward face/axis; minus is negative. U is the cube face normal closest to world up; F is the different face normal closest to the forward finger direction projected orthogonal to U. Exact ties use a deterministic axis-index order. These geometric definitions apply to any initial material face/color and yaw. Whole-cube axes beyond the listed transverse axis are outside this version's verified scope and will not be claimed.

For a layer request, exactly the four corners in its initial outward half-space must receive that quarter rotation; the other four keep their initial pose, subject to small whole-body grip drift defined below. For a whole-cube request, all eight corners receive the quarter rotation. The comparison uses all corner poses and their identities, so rotating only a core frame, a single corner, or the wrong half cannot count as the requested move. For scrambled starts, targets are computed from that actual starting arrangement, not from solved colors. Geometry and state frames will be documented with the simulation and export.

Evidence and normative choices

The video shows index upper-layer sweeps (frames 97–101, real 3.233–3.367 s, source-file 6.467–6.733 s) while the thumb braces lower near tiles; a right/far-half turn (86–90, real 2.867–3.0 s, file 5.733–6.0 s) while the left half is held; and a rapid roll/regrip around 242–246 (real 8.067–8.2 s, file 16.133–16.4 s). Lower fingers cradle the cube. No environment supports the active turns. The reconstruction measures the roll's geometric orientation relative to the fitted wrist as about 93.9° about (−0.7936, −0.5929, 0.1363) in the starting geometric object frame, wrist motion 31.3°. That is an uncertain blended-axis estimate: silhouette orientation has 24-fold symmetry, tracker sensitivity reaches roughly 49° at its 95th percentile, and additional layer motion appears by 246–247. It is not a recovered material-axis or exact signed move sequence. Layer states are unresolved. The exact cube-axis ±90° requests and final alignment come from TASK.md and override those ambiguities.

The fitted hand's active palm normal is typically 68.3° from up (5–95%: 55.3–109.5°); fingers elevate about 22.7° (14.5–52.2°). Wrist-to-cube centre distance is 0.132 m median (5–95%: 0.118–0.143 m). Active nominal cube clearance above the mat is positive. Fitted thumb proximity within 3 mm is about 95%; hidden support-finger identities are inferred. The final recorded fit passes contact, opposition, object penetration and finger-intersection checks; active hand frame 255 remains invalid. These estimates guide posture/contact roles, not exact execution trajectories.

The demonstrated fast turns span roughly 0.1–0.3 s. TASK.md prescribes a correct held move rather than a deadline. I impose an 8 s engineering episode cap, including a 0.5 s stable final hold, to allow deliberate contact establishment with different finger geometry. I do not claim the robot matches human speed. No measured speed threshold is silently relaxed: timing is a stated task-level choice before training.

Deployment coverage and physical uncertainty

Evaluation covers every listed move, all 24 face-up material orientations, continuous yaw, legal scrambled arrangements, and cube placement near the middle of the chosen palm. Nominal placement offsets are uniform within ±8 mm in the two palm directions and ±3 mm normal to the palm, with tilt within ±10°. Stress trials extend offsets to ±15 mm and tilt to ±20°. Cube size is varied over 49–52 mm, mass over 47–67 g, and external sliding friction over 0.3–1.0; internal resistance, contact compliance and damping receive explicit ranges in the simulation record. These are uncertain manufacturer-family/material priors, not measured exact-cube values. Every seed deterministically chooses its requested move and physical/start parameters. Dev seeds are frozen before policy comparisons. Fresh final seeds come from the gate. Report nominal and stress results, and per-move counts; finite sampling does not prove universal real-world success.

The initial wrist is elevated above the floor and oriented like the reconstructed hand, with palm normal 55–80° from up and the fingers inclined upward. The exact chosen pose and all 20 finger positions are recorded before training. No table, fixture or external contact may help the move. The final cube must remain in the hand; the demonstration's end release/tumble is deliberately excluded because TASK.md requires a held end.

Ordered stages and episode predicates

  1. hold: establish at least 0.25 s of an opposed grip (thumb and another finger), with the cube touching only the hand. No drop or environment contact occurs at any earlier time. The 0.25 s is shorter than the visible stable hold around frames 142–153 (~0.37 s) but spans many 1 kHz controller ticks and rejects a transient touch. Standard predicates: opposed_s >= 0.25, environment_contact_s <= 0.002. The evaluator additionally checks continuous hand support and cube integrity at physics-step resolution.
  2. move: achieve the requested corner permutation and orientations with at most 5° corner-orientation error and 1.5 mm corner-centre error after allowing the small grip drift described below. Layer alignment at completion is within 3°. A 3° error across a 25.5 mm half-width is about 1.34 mm, comparable to a small physical edge clearance; 5° is a separate net requested-turn tolerance, never a substitute for the tighter alignment check. No other layer may remain turned. For a whole-cube roll the root and all eight parts must turn consistently; standard predicates include opposed_rel_rotation_deg >= 67.5, passive_rel_rotation_deg <= 31.5, and goal_error_deg <= 15. The first two are 75% and 35% of a normative 90° roll, separating substantial finger-driven rotation from a wrist-only turn or passive slip. For layer turns the part-motion checks are computed directly from the simulated articulated corner states; the root may rotate when a fixed-to-core corner is in the moving layer, so a root-angle shortcut is invalid. Standard support predicates still apply to every move.
  3. settle: retain the completed requested state for the final 0.5 s, hand-supported, layers aligned within 3°, object centre speed ≤0.01 m/s and angular speed ≤0.10 rad/s, and no new wrong turn. These speed tolerances match the standard measurement definition of rest and express a stopped held cube. The evaluator checks that 0.5 s window directly; standard predicates include touching_hand_at_end >= 1, touching_environment_at_end <= 0, and episode-appropriate final-speed measurements. The cube centre stays within 0.17 m of the wrist (reference 95th percentile 0.143 m plus geometry/fit uncertainty). Its final centre drifts at most 20 mm from its initial hand-relative centre; this permits the visible regrip while keeping it in the same hand.

Across all stages: every finger joint obeys the authoritative position allowance, speed and effort limits on every physics step. Targets are finite, correctly shaped and clipped as specified. Any violation fails the episode. Maximum hand–object penetration must stay below 2 mm, and the cube must retain its connected, interlocked parts without divergent/illegal final configuration. If the mechanism approximation cannot represent breaking, state that limitation rather than claiming material strength is verified.

The wrist remains within 30 mm of its initial position and 40° of its initial orientation; its palm-normal angle remains in 45–110°. The rotation bound allows the reconstructed 31.3° regrip with modest estimation uncertainty while preventing a wrist-only 90° move. Standard predicates: wrist.path_length_m <= 0.15 and wrist.max_rotation_deg <= 40, with the tighter position/posture bounds checked directly. Joint motion and hand-relative object rotation must substantiate a finger-driven move. For layer turns, moving-part progress must coincide with moving finger joints and opposed support; direct part actuation, kinematic object replay and goal-dependent mechanism changes are forbidden.

A support gap longer than 4 ms fails; the 4 ms tolerance is two commanded wrist/finger periods and accommodates contact discretization, not a ballistic toss. Any cube contact with the floor fails. The standard environment predicate allows at most one 2 ms telemetry interval for numerical bookkeeping; the physics-step evaluator rejects actual assistance. No pass may be obtained through external contact.

Suites, rates and verdict

The task suite uses the three primitive families upper_twist, front_twist, and roll. Each has a feasibility suite (at least 0.25 success, with both requested signs represented) using any physically valid controller, and deployable skill suites for each sign (at least 0.80 success and zero hardware violations). Starts are generated by the deployment placement and any preceding held-start skill's actual endpoints, rather than only an artificial perfect grip. Each learned move is exported as a reusable skill. A composed goal-conditioned policy is evaluated from the deployment start after the primitive requirements are met.

An episode succeeds only when all three stages complete in order and all safety/style predicates hold. My empirical task pass rate is at least 0.90, with zero hardware-violation episodes. I require each exported move skill to meet its own 0.80 suite threshold, report every move's task counts and Wilson intervals, and disclose any poorly covered start region. The 0.90 target is a pre-training reliability choice for a one-move request; it is not an official criterion or proof of all possible placements. The final verdict names the task suite's fresh-seed final gate, the policy iteration, these criteria and all limitations. No target delivery applies to this task configuration.
