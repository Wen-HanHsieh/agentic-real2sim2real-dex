Criteria text

Agent verification criteria, version 1

These are my own simulation criteria, not an official evaluation. They follow TASK.md and this run's reconstructed video. The final verdict must name the registered criteria version, policy iteration and final task gate. A finite simulated sample cannot establish success for every real placement or model mismatch.

Goal and deployment starts

Solve the articulated 2x2 cube: every one of its six faces has a single color, all corners remain mechanically retained and correctly assembled, and the cube remains in the left hand throughout. The initial hand pose is chosen and documented with the simulation. The cube starts resting near the middle of that palm, with an independently generated short scramble and arbitrary face up and yaw. The video's table pickup and final release are context, not required deployment actions. A floor contact or a second-hand/environment-assisted solve fails.

Verification starts cover nontrivial 1–4 quarter-turn scrambles using all six faces, without immediate inverse cancellation; all 24 face-up cube orientations with continuous yaw, position perturbations up to 10 mm in the palm plane and 15 degrees of tilt. One quarter of starts are placement-stress cases up to 20 mm and 25 degrees. These are explicit interpretations of "a few turns", "roughly in the middle", "any side up" and imperfect placement; larger scrambles and more extreme placement remain limitations, not claimed coverage. Already solved controls may be evaluated separately, but do not substitute for scrambled trials.

Physical uncertainty includes cube edge 49–51 mm, mass 52–75 g, hand–cube sliding friction 0.5–1.2, internal cube friction/drag spanning a factor of two around the stated model, and small observation/action delays where implemented. These are deliberately broad priors: the video does not measure them. Each frozen suite specifies its distributions and fixed seeds. Hardware limits, actuator gains and encoder noise follow the supplied specification.

Measured demonstration and uncertainties

The derivative has 395 frames, 15 file fps and assumed 30 real fps, k/30 seconds. Left contact is estimated at frames 74–340. Manipulation frames 93–340 span 8.23 seconds. Final alignment occurs around 336–340, followed by release. The final visible yellow/green/orange faces look uniform; hidden faces are not observable, so all-face correctness comes from TASK.md.

In recorded reconstruction version 6 (r0014), the anatomical left palm-normal tilt from world up has 5th/median/95th percentiles 58.2/69.9/109.5 degrees during manipulation. The wrist's axiswise excursion is 47.7/29.0/50.8 mm; wrist speed median/95th percentile is 0.067/0.154 m/s. Cube–wrist distance has 5th/median/95th percentiles 112/129/148 mm. Whole-cube axiswise excursion is about 67/73/75 mm. Anatomical wrist axes use thumb side +X, back of left hand +Y, fingers −Z; the wrist is never mirrored. Finger direction is typically oblique (about 38–76 degrees from up), and the thumb-side axis points upward (about19–62degrees from up, median35degrees).

Fitted manipulation contact fractions are thumb 0.996, index 0.452, middle 0.440, ring 0.649, little 0.234, and opposed-grasp fraction over the full holding phase is 0.933. These are inferred consistency measurements after physical contact priors, not independently observed forces or exact hidden-finger labels. Exact layer turns and corner identities in the human sequence remain unresolved. Cube scale/mass are public priors. The short release trajectory uses inferred gravity/support corrections. These limitations prevent treating this reference as ground truth dynamics.

Ordered stages and episode success

  1. retain: Within the first 1 second, retain the placed cube for a continuous 0.5 second with its center within 0.20 m of the wrist. The 0.20 m bound includes the measured 112–148 mm distance, embodiment size differences and placement error. No environment support is allowed. The initial palm may face upward to accept placement.
  2. manipulate: Complete at least one required nontrivial layer turn by finger contact, continuing through the finite solution sequence with regrasping where needed. A completed quarter turn changes the four participating corners approximately 90 degrees relative to the supporting remainder and reaches the intended legal cube state within 5 degrees. A whole-wrist rotation, passive slip or programmatically changed cube state does not count. The 5-degree alignment allowance is an engineering tolerance for compliant contact and blurred final-edge evidence, not a measured exact turn-state error. All motion commands must be produced by the reported PPO-trained network; a finite high-level cube solver may select subgoals from allowed part states.
  3. solved_hold: All six faces are uniform and the maximum corner pose error to a legal solved configuration is at most 5 degrees for a continuous 1 second while still held. During this interval cube linear speed is at most 0.03 m/s and angular speed at most 0.30 rad/s. These deliberately require a stable outcome beyond the video's brief held alignment and exclude a transient crossing of the solved state. The episode allowance is 30 seconds, an explicit engineering assumption for varying scrambles, capture and robot hardware; it is not the measured human duration.

Every successful episode must also meet all of the following throughout:

  • No cube–floor or other fixed-object contact, no second hand, and no hand-contact gap longer than 50 ms. Fifty milliseconds permits isolated soft-contact solver transitions across fingertip seams, not a toss. Any unsupported flight over that gap fails even if caught.
  • The cube remains inside a 0.20 m wrist-centered retention radius; all corner centers stay on the mechanism's retained geometry. No illegal isolated corner twist, piece escape or state teleport is accepted. The simulator must expose its mechanism and integrity checks; rigid-envelope success is insufficient.
  • During active manipulation after capture, at least 90% of samples have palm-normal tilt 45–135 degrees from up and finger-direction tilt 20–100 degrees from up, with the thumb-side +X axis within90degrees of up. The approximately 15–28 degree margins around the reconstructed ranges account for monocular uncertainty and robot anatomy, while still requiring the demonstrated oblique wrist. Wrist excursion from the post-capture anchor is at most 0.15 m. This is roughly three times the measured axiswise excursion, allowing placement adaptation while preserving the compact wrist motion. Absolute yaw/location may adapt to the placed cube.
  • At least 80% of active-turn time has thumb and at least one other finger touching the cube, and the driving fingers move. This follows the demonstrated stabilizing thumb and finger flicks, supported by the fitted opposed fraction 0.933, with an explicit allowance for regrasp transitions. Contact forces are used only for evaluation/training labels, never as deployable observations.
  • No hardware violation at any 1 kHz physics/controller tick: actual joint positions within the specified allowance, velocities within the vendor maxima and applied motor torques within effort limits. Network outputs and target shapes must be finite and valid. PD saturation is logged separately.

Stages are reported in order; later stages cannot rescue a failed earlier stage or a continuous constraint violation. Solved geometry without demonstrated handling fails the skill criteria.

Measurements, suites and pass rule

The task suite uses 64 distinct frozen development seeds and a final gate of 128 fresh seeds. The required pass rate is at least 90%, with zero hardware-violation episodes. This is deliberately a reliability claim over varied starts rather than acceptance of a single replay, and will not be relaxed after failures. Report confidence intervals, placement/scramble strata, individual constraint failures and representative successful/failed videos.

The standard meas-2 log records one cube root, wrist, all finger joints and actual contacts. held_s, hand_contact_s, opposed_s, end hand/environment contact, final-second speeds and wrist motion provide independent gate guardrails. The frozen evaluator additionally records all eight part poses/velocities and computes legal layer progress, solved color arrangement, integrity, continuous support, phase-specific wrist tilt and excursion from those states. Standard meas-2 has no part-state or wrist-tilt measure; this limitation is explicit, and root rotation is never used as a surrogate for a layer turn. Custom predicates and their formulas remain inspectable in the frozen evaluator and supplemental state arrays.

Task primitives are retention, layer_turn, and regrasp. Before whole-task training/gates, prove each with actual hand-controlled physical rollouts in a registered feasibility suite. A feasibility controller may be scripted or privileged and must be labelled separately; it is never the final PPO policy. Any separately trained skill needs its own registered skill suite and downstream starts drawn from prior incumbent outcomes.

A pass is only a claim about this simulation and the stated finite start/model distribution. Unresolved real mechanism details, exact cube identity, contact compliance, state-estimation accuracy and untested starts remain limitations even if the final gate passes.
