Agent verification criteria, version 1

These are my own simulation criteria, not an official score or a claim about real hardware. They are written before building/evaluating the robot simulation. The reference is reconstruction version 5, r0012, in research/recon_phys_ray; task intent is TASK.md.

Evidence and interpretation

The goal is one requested top-layer quarter turn, clockwise or counterclockwise viewed from above, with the cube held throughout and every layer aligned at the end. Solving the entire scramble, pickup from a table, and the demonstrated final release are outside this task. The commanded face is selected at the request time: the cube face with greatest outward-normal world-up component. Its four original corner pieces remain the selected layer even if the grip adjusts.

The video is a slowed derivative: frame k is k/30 seconds of real time (file time k/15). Upper-band turn candidates are94:99,103:107,310:315,334:337. The last has a unique upper white pair moving front-to-right while the lower green pair stays front, supporting an inferred counterclockwise quarter turn between aligned endpoints. Continuous internal part states are not reconstructed. Both signed90-degree goals come from the instruction; reverse-direction execution must be learned even where the video does not isolate it.

In310:315(10.333–10.500s real), the fitted anatomical wrist rotates about3.9degrees, translates about4.6mm, and the index tip moves about38.8mm. The wrist pose has palm-out normal about68.5degrees from world up and thumb-side axis about31.5degrees from up. The index sweeps an upper/back edge, while thumb and lower fingers keep the cube. Exact hidden contacts are inferred. Reference hand–cube penetration is1.63mm maximum, opposed-contact fraction87.5%, with no detected finger intersections or continuity flags in the latest check. These quality checks are conditional on the fit, not ground truth. Cropped right-hand reprojection remains a warning, and shared absolute-depth sensitivity is about90mm. Rigid-envelope rotations are not layer angles.

Starts and coverage

The hand has one chosen, documented start wrist/finger pose. Cube starts must be physically at rest and supported by that hand with its pose held fixed, as the deployment describes. A task-suite start check uses the fixed hold policy and support=hand; at least95% of64 fresh starts must pass the harness's1s check. A failed start distribution is corrected through the receiving hand pose or physically meaningful placement, never by declaring environment support.

Evaluation samples both requests with equal probability; all six upward faces, arbitrary yaw on[0,2pi), legal scramble states, and placement offsets up to10mm in each horizontal direction about the palm's receiving region. Small placement tilt is sampled in a cone of20degrees about the nominal upward face. A cube deposited in the hand may settle physically before episode time zero under the same fixed hold policy; no object is teleported into a grasp or reset through interpenetration. All sampling and settling are deterministic from the seed and logged. The gate's starts and handoff states are separate from training starts.

Object priors cover edge48–52mm, mass50–90g, hand–plastic friction0.4–1.2, restitution0–0.2, and uncertain internal resistance/detents. These are explicit uncertainty ranges, not measurements of the demonstration cube. Their implementation and any omitted dimension are stated with the simulation version. Any side up and scramble are represented by legal corner permutations/orientations; if internal geometry is treated as symmetric, that is a limitation and colors alone do not count as testing state-dependent dynamics.

An episode lasts at most10s. The instruction gives no deadline;10s allows safe robot grip adjustment and a slow turn relative to the0.10–0.17s visible human sweeps. Slower execution is an explicit deviation while preserving finger contact and wrist style.

Per-episode goal and integrity

  1. The signed, unwrapped relative angle of the selected upper layer changes by exactly s*pi/2 within2degrees, where s=-1 means clockwise and s=+1 counterclockwise about its outward normal. Total angular travel is at most120degrees and excursion opposite the requested direction at most10degrees. Three opposite turns, a whole-cube spin, and merely changing a color label do not qualify. Every unrequested articulation stays within10degrees of its initial aligned configuration and ends within2degrees of an aligned state.
  2. Final layer alignment and requested angle persist for0.5s. Every part's relative angular speed is below0.1rad/s during that interval; cube base speed is below0.01m/s and angular speed below0.1rad/s throughout the final1s. The cube touches the hand at the end. The2-degree tolerance corresponds to about0.87mm lateral offset at25mm radius, comparable to rounded edges/clearance rather than an intentionally misaligned layer. It is from an engineering alignment tolerance, not a resolved video angle. Quiet speeds are the harness rest thresholds;0.5s is longer than the roughly0.3s visible held pause137:146 and tests actual stabilization.
  3. The cube touches no floor, table, fixed fixture, or other object during the episode. It is hand-supported for at least98% of the episode, with no gap in hand contact longer than20ms. At20ms, free fall is about2mm, so this allowance handles contact switching at compliant pads rather than a toss. The final0.5s is continuously held. A drop is failure even if later recovered.
  4. All corner pieces remain retained and connected according to the modeled mechanism. No part separates by more than2mm from its permitted mechanism configuration, no undocumented joint/constraint is removed, and no geometry passes deeply through the hand or another part. Maximum hand–cube penetration is2mm in the recorded rollout. The model must expose mechanism loads; per-contact hand–cube normal force must not exceed20N and selected-layer constraint torque0.10Nm. These load bounds are conservative engineering assumptions to reject force exploits, not measured fracture limits. If retention is idealized, verification cannot establish real anti-pop or fracture resistance; that limitation must be reported.
  5. Every tick obeys the actual Wuji20-joint position, speed and applied-effort limits and controller specification. Zero episodes with a hardware violation are allowed for any passing suite. Saturation is reported separately from violations.

Finger skill and wrist pose

During the layer motion and final stabilization, the palm-out normal is53.5–83.5degrees from world up (68.5degree reference plus/minus15degrees), thumb-side axis is within45degrees of up (31.5degree reference plus13.5degrees fitting/retargeting allowance), and fingers point forward with an upward elevation between0 and45degrees (about22degrees in the fitted reference). Global heading may change with the robot environment. The wrist rotates at most15degrees from its initial pose and travels at most50mm in the entire episode. The rotation bound admits the measured3.9degree change plus uncertain hand fitting, while ruling out a wrist-powered quarter turn. Translation is free in deployment;50mm is one cube width and keeps the wrist close to its receiving location.

The thumb and at least one other finger oppose the cube for at least80% of the articulation interval. At least75% of accumulated upper-layer rotation must occur while an index or middle finger contacts that upper layer and its joint-speed norm exceeds0.3rad/s. The reconstructed index sweep is about0.23m/s average, well above a passive hold; the speed threshold is the harness passive-motion threshold. Up to25% passive finishing permits detent alignment, not a full passive turn. Contacts and forces may be used by training/evaluation, but never observed by the deployable policy. The physical actor uses only the permitted proprioception, object/part states, requested move and history.

Where the robot needs a different contact placement because its anatomy differs, document it before changing a criterion. Goal completion has priority, but replacing finger articulation with wrist rotation or an external fixture is unacceptable. A criterion revision is a new recorded version with evidence and reason, never a relabeling of old results.

Ordered stages and chain

The chain is settle_grip from deployment start, followed alternatively by turn_cw or turn_ccw. Each turn primitive includes alignment and final rest, so no hidden terminal transition is omitted. Its start states come from the actual end states of settle_grip. The whole-task stages are:

  • grip: attain a supported, opposed lower-layer grip for0.5s before meaningful layer motion. Standard checks: opposed_s>=0.5, environment_contact_s<=0, touching_hand_at_end=1 in its primitive suite; evaluator checks timing and layer drift.
  • turn: complete the selected signed articulation with the support, active-finger, style and integrity conditions above. Standard checks: environment_contact_s<=0, opposed_rel_translation_m<=0.04, wrist.max_rotation_deg<=15, wrist.path_length_m<=0.05; evaluator checks logged selected-layer angles, part contacts, active-motion fraction and loads. No whole-object rotation measurement substitutes for articulation.
  • aligned_rest: all goal, alignment and quiet-hold conditions persist at the end. Standard checks: touching_hand_at_end>=1, touching_environment_at_end<=0, last_second_max_speed_m_s<=0.01, last_second_max_angular_speed_rad_s<=0.1; evaluator checks all part velocities/angles and the0.5s interval.

State logs include the standard harness keys, simulator state, commanded targets, requested sign, selected original face/corner identities, every part state and internal angle/velocity, contact membership by layer/finger, peak loads and penetrations. The suite runs deterministically and reports stages in order. Harness predicates supplement these explicit articulation checks; the harness currently has no standard internal-layer-angle measurement.

Pass rates and evidence

Feasibility suites require at least25% success with any hardware-compliant controller, from the chain's handoff states, before whole-task training. Each separately learned deployable skill requires at least80% success without hardware violations and is frozen in the skill library. These establish that both requested turns have a usable policy.

The final whole-task criterion is at least90% complete episode success on the task suite's fresh final starts, with zero hardware-violation episodes, plus both directional skills mastered at80% or better.90% demands substantially more reliability than a one-off demonstration or a single successful rollout; a policy ignoring one request cannot pass. Report actual counts and Wilson intervals, separately for each request, upward face and placement/physics range. Do not claim coverage beyond sampled ranges.

Choose the checkpoint on development results only, run its final task gate once, and report that gate's verdict and limitations with top/left/right success and failure videos, logs and policy/scene hashes. Any final failure remains a failure; it is a reason to diagnose and continue within the time budget. Passing these criteria establishes only my claim about this training simulation, not official or real-world performance.
