Criteria text

Agent verification criteria v1: four-turn pen pass

These are my own criteria for this simulation, not an official evaluation. They are fixed before policy training or evaluation. The evidence is TASK.md, the 180-frame 30 Hz demonstration, and the recorded reference. The reference is an uncertain kinematic estimate, not a force measurement. Its extra between-frame check still reports up to 7.5 mm pen-axis penetration into interpolated MANO surfaces; the learned policy must execute under rigid-body dynamics and may not replay those penetrations.

Task understanding and ordered plan

The right hand starts holding the pen between index and middle fingers. It makes four clockwise half-turns while transferring support to middle–ring, ring–pinky, middle–ring, and index–middle, then holds the pen. The thumb stays free. The wrist remains in the demonstrated palm orientation and makes small translations and rotations. The pen is not placed on the background desk or floor.

  1. Establish index–middle support.
  2. Complete one clockwise half-turn and establish middle–ring support.
  3. Complete a second clockwise half-turn and establish ring–pinky support.
  4. Complete a third clockwise half-turn and return to middle–ring support.
  5. Complete a fourth clockwise half-turn and return to index–middle support.
  6. Keep the pen in a settled index–middle hold.

The task instruction determines the required pair order and nominal angles. Visible tip and ring-colour evidence supports it. Exact contact forces and some occluded finger surfaces are inferred. Preparatory finger movements may begin before each main rotation.

Measurements and uncertainty

Recorded reference v3 (r0012; retaining v2 measurements unchanged by the two-frame depth correction) measures 711.8 degrees clockwise in the image and 719.0 degrees clockwise in the reconstructed world horizontal heading. The stable-pair/turn endpoints are near frames 0, 48, 88, 151, and 174 (0, 1.600, 2.933, 5.033, 5.800 s). The four main turn windows are frames 33–47, 74–87, 117–151, and 158–173, lasting 0.500, 0.467, 1.167, and 0.533 s. Holds between transfers last approximately 1.10, 0.87, 0.97, 0.20, and 0.20 s.

The reconstructed wrist spans approximately 10.2 cm translation from its initial position and 21.0 degrees rotation. The reconstruction estimates 15–25 mm position uncertainty and 20–35 degrees pen-axis uncertainty in holds/turns; these are heuristic uncertainty assignments, not calibrated confidence intervals. Pen axial roll is unobservable. Monocular focal/depth, gravity and camera-translation ambiguities remain. Final 0.20 s of video has about 27.3 degrees reconstructed axis change and 17.7 mm pen-to-wrist movement, so the clip does not establish a fully settled terminal hold. The explicit instruction “Then hold it” motivates the additional settled-hold condition below.

Evaluation protocol

Evaluate 20 full episodes from the demonstrated initial phase, with fixed seeds 202600–202619. Each starts with the simulation record's retargeted initial pose, independent uniform relative pen translation in ±2 mm per axis, pen rotation in ±2 degrees per axis, and finger positions in ±0.01 rad clipped within limits. Wrist initial pose is the recorded reference pose. Use the nominal specified pen mass/inertia, gravity and nominal simulation contact parameters. Encoder noise remains enabled. No mid-motion resets, target-state overwrites, object assistance or evaluation-time planning are allowed.

Run for 6.5 s. The actor is a fixed checkpoint, acts closed-loop at 500 Hz, and observes only the deployable observation set in LEARNING.md. Rewards, contacts, forces and physical parameters are available only to training/verification code, never to the actor. Record all raw states, contact labels, controller targets and hardware violations. Evaluate identical criteria for the base reference controller as a diagnostic; a replay is not a PPO solution.

Quantities and pass conditions

  1. Physical validity and retention. The pen remains a free rigid body driven only by gravity and collisions. There are no welds, attachments, kinematic object updates, or object force/pose assistance. It never touches the floor. Thumb or palm contact carrying more than 0.05 N must occupy less than 5% of the episode. This excludes sustained support absent from the video while tolerating solver-scale incidental touches. Every finger joint meets the position allowance, strict speed and effort limits of hardware/SPECS.md at every 1 kHz physics tick. All actor outputs are finite and valid. Any violation fails the episode.
  1. Directed rotation. Let the pen's state-frame +X be the directed axis toward the red end. Compute clockwise angle as minus the continuously unwrapped azimuth of this axis projected onto world XY, relative to its initial azimuth. Evaluate at 500 Hz; the horizontal projection norm must stay at least 0.25 (reference minimum about 0.35), avoiding a near-vertical heading singularity. There are four ordered milestone angles, 180, 360, 540 and 720 degrees, each within ±25 degrees. The tolerance covers the approximately 20-degree axis estimate in holds and the small endpoint offsets from nominal half-turns. The final net angle must be in [695, 745] degrees. Maximum backward drawdown from the largest angle already reached is at most 75 degrees: the reference has approximately 51 degrees image and 60 degrees smoothed world drawdown, so this allows preparatory reversals but excludes an extra reversed half-turn.
  1. Ordered finger-pair support. Label contacts by pen geometry and the anatomical finger link subtree. A finger is in contact only if at least one solved collision has distance ≤1 mm and summed compressive normal force ≥0.01 N. The 1 mm is a contact-model tolerance, substantially smaller than the 9 mm barrel; 0.01 N is about 5% of pen weight and rejects zero-force proximity. Both members of a pair must satisfy this condition simultaneously for 0.08 s continuously. This is shorter than the shortest observed 0.20 s hold but longer than two video frames. First establish index–middle support at the initial angle by 0.25 s (the video already starts held; this allows simulated contact/controller settling); then establish middle–ring, ring–pinky, middle–ring, index–middle at their respective angle milestones, in that order. An angle milestone without the corresponding pair does not count. Contacts may overlap during transfers.
  1. Timing. Pair/angle milestones must complete within ±0.25 s of 1.60, 2.93, 5.03, and 5.80 s. The quarter-second tolerance covers approximately two frames of phase-boundary ambiguity plus motor/controller settling and small differences between human and robot finger proportions. All four must finish by 6.05 s, leaving time for the final hold. The reference timings are not silently slowed to make a policy pass.
  1. Wrist resemblance. Use the reconstructed anatomical wrist frame: +X toward the thumb side, −Z toward middle-finger MCP, +Y out of the right palm. The simulation record fixes the mapping from this frame to r_mount and a constant scene translation before training; no per-rollout alignment is fitted. Over 0–6.5 s, compare the executed wrist pose with this reference at the same real time, holding its last wrist pose constant after the 6 s clip. Translation RMS error must be ≤30 mm and 95th percentile ≤50 mm. Rotation geodesic RMS error must be ≤15 degrees and 95th percentile ≤30 degrees. The three initial annotation-noise refits changed wrist orientation by less than 4 degrees; the recovered outlier reached 11.5 degrees maximum (5.1 degrees at the 95th percentile). Wrist position varied by up to 37 mm in the poorer local minimum. These tolerances account for the monocular placement error and the different wrist origins while preserving the observed palm orientation and approximately 21-degree motion range. They prevent replacing finger turns with large wrist spins. Report finger-joint tracking and object-path errors as additional diagnostics, without requiring exact human joint motion.
  1. Final hold. After the fourth milestone, sustain index–middle contact throughout the terminal 0.30 s window [6.20, 6.50] s. Over that interval, maximum pen-centre displacement relative to the wrist from its interval-start value is ≤10 mm, and maximum directed-axis angular deviation is ≤15 degrees. The 10 mm is approximately one barrel diameter; 15 degrees limits continued turning. These are explicit operational assumptions for “hold,” stricter than the uncertain clipped terminal video, rather than thresholds selected from a policy's performance. Axial roll is reported separately because the demonstration does not determine it.

An episode succeeds only if all six conditions hold, with all pair transitions in order. Report partial progress as the number of completed ordered turn/pair milestones (0–4), never as success. The policy passes this self-verification only with at least 18 successful episodes out of 20, and zero hardware violations in every episode counted as successful. Report the complete success count, per-criterion measurements and failures; the 90% requirement is an explicit reliability target for this small simulated perturbation set.

Evidence and limitations

Publish raw metric files and top, left and right rollout videos of a representative successful episode and a failed episode whenever one exists. Include checkpoint and scene hashes, seeds, noise and perturbations. A passing result establishes only performance in this chosen simulator under this protocol. It does not establish real-robot transfer, exact human contact forces, physical parameter identification, or an official task score.
