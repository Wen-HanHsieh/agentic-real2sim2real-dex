Evidence and interpretation

Hidden exact contact points are inferred. The robot may use a stable common pinch and a slower/fixed wrist posture within this envelope. Any geometric or kinematic deviation is recorded; the instruction's finger-driven turn and steady hold decide.

Deployment envelope

Each episode has exactly one already-held plug, object_kind0 white-like or1 black-like, with the Blender mesh/state frames of the reference (+z points out through blades). Both kinds occur on fresh starts. The wrist/finger start pose and supported object-in-grasp distribution are chosen and recorded with the simulation. Starts must be genuinely stable under the stationary hold policy and pass the harness start check; they cannot be falling catches or pre-oriented successes. Initial blade-axis error is at least50° from world up. Train/test translations span at least±3mm and orientations at least±10° around the chosen grasp, subject to physical support. State the actual accepted envelope and any rejected impossible starts.

Evaluate physical uncertainty in each gate: at minimum object mass0.8–1.2 times its nominal prior, casing/rubber sliding friction0.4–1.0, COM offsets up to3mm, and casing dimensions within±5% of their prior. Exact distributions and correlations are frozen in the simulation/evaluator. Also report separate diagnostic starts with wider placement, mass/friction/geometry perturbations; disclose every limitation. Randomization is not an observed policy input.

Ordered stages and episode success

  1. retained: The object remains supported by the hand, with a thumb and at least one opposing finger touching during a sustained interval. At least0.25s of opposed contact is required, an explicit settling tolerance. The object never touches the table, floor, another object or any fixed support during the task episode. Hand/environment bracing is also excluded by the evaluator. Initial stability is judged independently by the harness hold-policy start check.
  2. turned: Within one opposed interval, the object changes orientation relative to the wrist by at least40°. This is approximately75% of the measured54° black turn, allowing monocular uncertainty while excluding a wrist-only solution. The primitive gate adds its own threshold from the exact recorded reference angle. Passive relative rotation is at most15°, using the standard0.1s window/0.30rad/s joint-speed indicator. Because that indicator alone can miss unrelated finger motion, the evaluator additionally requires at least0.15rad of commanded target motion in a thumb/index/middle digit that actually contacts the object during the turn, and at least4mm of actual motion of a contacting fingertip relative to the wrist. These are explicit conservative activity assumptions; non-contact finger wiggling does not qualify. A representative rollout is visually inspected against the human turn.
  3. steady: The blade +z axis is within15° of world +z for the final1.0s, the object is still touching the hand at the end and is held in opposition for at least90% of that final second. Over that second, maximum object linear speed is<=0.02m/s and maximum angular speed<=0.20rad/s. The tolerances express visibly upright/steady behavior with small contact compliance;15° is materially smaller than the turning motion. The final relative orientation is within25° of a blades-up goal, with yaw explicitly free (a finite dense set of equivalent goal quaternions in the log).

All stages must occur in order, without dropping or losing the plug. Wrist rotation from its starting orientation is<=35°, based on the largest reconstructed wrist contribution. Its palm normal remains downward/oblique (<=80° from down), and its path length is<=0.15m; these explicit assumptions preserve the demonstrated posture and prevent carrying motions from substituting for the turn. A fixed wrist is allowed and makes the fingers responsible for the rotation. No finger hardware violation is allowed at any physics tick: positions within URDF limits plus0.02rad, speeds and applied torques within the specified limits; targets must be finite and correctly shaped.

The episode length/control rate is frozen with the simulation. It must allow turning followed by the full1s hold; failure cannot terminate early and be counted as a steady success. State logs include actual contacts, joint targets, full simulator state and velocities for independent measurement and reproducible handoffs. Contact information is evaluator/training evidence only, never a deployable policy observation.
