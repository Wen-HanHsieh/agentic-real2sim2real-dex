Plug manipulation: my simulation verification criteria, version 1

These are my own criteria, not an official evaluation or score. They are written before any policy is judged. A passing verdict is a finite simulation claim under the conditions below; it cannot establish success for every real plug or every possible start.

Evidence and interpretation

TASK.md requires picking two or three plugs up one at a time from the right, placing every plug on the left with its blades up, and clearing the right. TASK_CONFIG defines the side by housing centre: left is world y > +0.15 m. It permits either charger kind and touching/leaning starts. The episode is 30 seconds and policy calls are at 500 Hz.

The video shows a thumb/opposing-finger pinch, lift, turn, carry, base contact, opening and withdrawal, repeated for the second plug. Contact intervals estimated from the video are white frames 69–215 and black 248–386; final free-standing observation is approximately 7.33 s for white and 1.63 s for black. The reconstruction estimates horizontal transports of 0.197 and 0.200 m, with maximum lowest-mesh-point clearances of 18 and 51 mm. These are uncertain monocular estimates. Final zero-degree tilt in the reference is an inferred upright constraint, not an independent measurement. Visible blades/base support establish the upright interpretation.

Per-episode criteria

All of the following must hold for every present object, whether there are two or three. Objects keep their identities throughout the episode; object count or kind cannot be inferred from the demonstration's fixed pair.

  1. A real, sequential pickup precedes placement. Each plug must have an interval of at least 0.10 s with hand-object contact, no table contact, and its lowest collision point at least 5 mm above both the tabletop and its initial lowest-point height. During a pickup/carry, at most one plug may simultaneously have gained at least 5 mm of clearance and lost table support. Initial leaning alone does not count as a pickup. This distinguishes the instructed pickup from pushing or carrying a pair together. The 5 mm clearance is below the estimated demonstration clearances, yet exceeds the 3 mm geometry/contact tolerance; 0.10 s is 50 policy steps and rejects a contact bounce.
  1. All housing centres finish on the left. For the entire final 1.0 s (t in [29,30] s), every housing centre has y > +0.15 m. No particular order or exact final XY arrangement is required. This is the task's stated side boundary; no substitute video-specific target position is used.
  1. Every plug stands on its base, blades upward. Throughout the final 1.0 s, dot(R_object * [0,0,1], [0,0,1]) >= cos(10 degrees), the minimum point of the housing's base is within 3 mm of z=0, and the plug has table contact. The +Z state axis follows the blades. Ten degrees allows a small geometric/estimation tilt around the clearly upright demonstrated outcome and is substantially below a box's geometric tipping angle for the estimated dimensions. The 3 mm allowance covers fitted base geometry and compliant numerical contact; it does not allow a visibly floating or buried housing.
  1. Release and independent stability. There is no hand-object contact during the final 1.0 s. Each object's speed stays below 0.01 m/s and angular speed below 0.10 rad/s in that interval. At 1 cm/s a housing would move 1 cm in a second, a substantial fraction of its 22–42 mm base; these bounds require actual settling. The 1 s duration is shorter than the observed 1.63 s unheld tail of the last demonstration plug. In an additional test initialized from the final state, remove the hand and all other objects and simulate each plug alone under gravity for 1 s with unchanged properties: it must remain on its base within the same position/tilt tolerances. This rejects support by a hidden finger or a leaning neighbour while allowing plugs to touch in the actual final arrangement.
  1. Physical and hardware validity. The commanded action is finite and conforms to contract v2. Finger positions, velocities and applied torques satisfy every rule of hardware/SPECS.md at every 1 kHz controller tick (position allowance 0.02 rad; no speed/torque allowance). Wrist targets use the specified workspace, rate limits and impedance controller. No plug falls from the table. Contacts are generated by the simulator; no attachment, teleportation, gravity cancellation, hidden object force or scripted success state is allowed. The policy receives only the deployable observations allowed by LEARNING.md, including the task's object_kind key. Any policy exception, timeout, numerical instability or hardware violation makes the episode fail.

Episode success means all five criteria hold in order. A placement is not credited before that object's pickup. Progress counts completed objects, but partial progress is not success.

Evaluation set and randomization

The policy verdict will use 128 held-out episodes with seeds 45000–45127, disjoint from training/reset seeds, stratified equally between two and three objects. Within each count, cover all kind mixtures, initially lying blade directions, widely separated starts, touching/leaning starts, table edges and corners, and changed physical properties. There are 32 broad nominal episodes, 32 randomized-physics episodes, 32 contact/leaning episodes, and 32 edge/corner episodes. Every test runs the full 30 s; an early completed arrangement must remain stable to the end.

Starts cover the configured tabletop and its right side, using the actual randomized meshes to keep the initial support footprint on the table; they are not restricted to the video or a convenient wrist neighbourhood. Orientations are drawn before a gravity settle, with explicit stable side-face and blade-up/down cases also included. Contact/leaning starts are produced by nearby drops and settled physical contact. The hand holds its chosen fixed start pose while objects settle for 1.0 s, then for another 0.5 s with less than 3 mm drift, matching DELIVERY.md. All generation/rejection counts and final measured starts are saved. An inability to reach an otherwise valid deployment start is a failure/limitation, not an excluded episode.

Verification physical variations include independently scaled body dimensions (0.85–1.15), blade dimensions (0.9–1.1), mass (0.6–1.4 times its family prior), COM offsets up to 4 mm per axis, inertia scale 0.7–1.3, object/table sliding friction 0.25–1.0, rubber friction 0.6–1.4, and stable contact time constants 3–12 ms. Exact sampled values and distributions are recorded with the simulation. Hardware controllers/limits and specified encoder noise are preserved. The ranges represent uncertain family geometry and unobserved physical properties, not measurements of the real objects.

A policy-level PASS requires success in all 128 episodes, with every per-criterion metric and failure retained. Training diagnostics may use smaller fixed sets but do not count as this verification. If an evaluation implementation or initial-condition generator is wrong, correct it, disclose the correction and rerun; do not relax a threshold to pass a policy. Representative top/left/right videos, all initial states, per-step validity summaries, per-object pickup/placement times and final stability tests accompany the verdict.

Limitations include finite sampling, uncertain real geometry/materials, exact object-state observations, approximate contact physics and the absence of real hardware trials. Target rounds, if reached, are judged separately against the same object-level goal and hardware criteria; placement failures or policy failures are recorded explicitly and are not called successful trials.
