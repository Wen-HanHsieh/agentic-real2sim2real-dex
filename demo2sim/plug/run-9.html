Criteria text

Plug manipulation: agent verification criteria, v1

These are my own simulation criteria, not an official score. This version is recorded before building the robot simulation or training any policy. Evidence comes from TASK.md, the 30fps demonstration and the recorded reconstruction; uncertain reconstruction quantities are used as broad motion-style envelopes, not exact trajectories.

Goal and ordered per-object subgoals

The instruction requires each of two or three side-lying charger adapters to be picked individually from the right, turned blades upward and stood on the left. The video shows opposing thumb/finger contact, airborne reorientation and carry, then supported placement and release. White is handled first in the example; arbitrary order is allowed. The blades remain rigidly attached; there is no observed plug mechanism.

  1. Approach and grasp. For each selected plug, thumb and at least one other finger contact its case for at least 50ms before the airborne carry. A continuous 50ms interval avoids counting a contact impulse; contacts are evaluator-only data and are never policy observations. The non-selected plugs may touch one another at the start.
  2. Lift, turn and carry. Every plug's lowest mesh point must rise at least 15mm above the tabletop for at least 100ms and its body center must pass from y<=0.15 to y>0.15 while held. During the airborne transfer it must contact the hand for at least 80% of samples. The 15mm clearance is a conservative threshold below the visibly lifted body heights, safely above normal contact compliance; 100ms is five frames at the demonstration's time resolution. No two plugs may be simultaneously elevated more than15mm for longer than50ms. This implements one-at-a-time handling.
  3. Stand and release. By30s all plug centers must have y>0.16m (10mm margin beyond the specified side boundary), blades within15degrees of world+Z, the flat case base supported by the table with its lowest point within[-2,+3]mm of z=0, and no hand-object contact. Each plug must remain so for the last1s, with linear speed below0.02m/s and angular speed below0.15rad/s. The tilt tolerance allows small modeling uncertainty while requiring standing on the case base. The contact bands cover compliant contact and numerical tolerances, not visible floating. No plug may fall off the table.
  4. Hand and wrist motion. Approach/grasp palm normal points generally downward (median dot with world+Z<=-0.4). A lift precedes the cross-table transfer, the object becomes upright before final release, and the hand opens to release. The actor's wrist rotation is compared with the anatomical wrist reference reconstructed from MANO, using one object-yaw alignment at grasp and event-aligned grasp/turn/carry/place phases. Map each measured phase's elapsed fraction linearly onto the reference interval and interpolate rotations by shortest-arc SLERP over valid frames; do not refit alignment per frame. The white reference intervals are[74,97],[98,138],[139,170],[171,214], and black[250,281],[282,332],[333,353],[354,384]. Omit invalid reference frames and report their fraction. Report median and90th-percentile geodesic wrist-orientation error, and palm-normal traces. For a successful episode require median<=45degrees and90th-percentile<=75degrees for each plug, and the approach condition. Broad orientation bands acknowledge monocular depth, occlusion, retargeting anatomy and alternative object yaw while rejecting a wholly different wrist strategy. Show videos alongside these numbers; the threshold alone cannot establish human likeness.
  5. Hardware and physical integrity. Every finger sample respects the position allowance, URDF speed limits and applied effort limits in hardware/SPECS.md. The wrist uses the specified impedance controller and command rate limits. Report torque-demand saturation separately. Report maximum contact penetration; sustained hand-object/table penetration above5mm for more than20ms fails an episode. No attachment, artificial object force, kinematic object repositioning or gravity change is permitted during policy rollouts.

An episode succeeds only if every criterion holds, in order for all its objects. Partial progress is reported separately as number of correctly standing released objects; it is not episode success.

Deployment distributions and evaluation

Evaluate a deterministic checkpoint on60 independently seeded episodes that were not used for PPO updates or selection:30 with two plugs and30 with three, balanced across both kinds. Within each count use10 separated,10 touching/leaning and10 scattered edge-of-reach layouts. Sample random resting orientations and yaw by dropping rigid plugs onto the table and settling them before the policy starts; do not simply replay the demonstration's two orientations. Positions span the modeled reachable right-side table, with exact ranges recorded in the simulation version. Include repeats of each kind, arbitrary object order and initial object-object contact. Run the documented1.5s placement/settle sequence with the chosen hand start pose.

Randomize mass[0.7,1.3]x nominal, white-case dimensions[0.9,1.1]x nominal, and black-case X[18,26]mm/Y[27,37]mm/Z[42,52]mm (covering the black-shape fit ambiguity and extra model uncertainty), COM offsets up to3mm per axis, table/hand-object friction[0.3,1.0], restitution[0,0.15], and encoder noise as specified. Test the object models and controllers on independent seeds; record any implementation limitation or narrower range actually supported.

A policy passes my verification at>=90% episode success overall, >=85% for each object count and >=80% in the touching/leaning stratum, with zero hardware violations in all60 episodes. These thresholds state a practical robustness claim over a finite test, not universal success or target success. Include a binomial confidence interval, all per-episode/per-object metrics, seeds and initial states. Preserve top/left/right videos for a success and a failure where present.

Ambiguities and limitations

Exact charger identities, mass distribution and friction are not visible. Public product measurements are priors, and randomization does not establish that target objects fall within those ranges. Full-table placement may include points beyond the finite wrist workspace plus finger reach; explicitly report the reachable evaluation envelope and any unsupported starts. Camera translation, fully covered object rotations and some finger contacts are unresolved in the reconstruction. Wrist comparisons must identify invalid reference frames and the exact frame alignment; no missing frame may count as a perfect match. The video provides one example and cannot prove coverage of arbitrary orientations, touching layouts or three plugs. No criterion here is an official evaluation, and the target receives only a policy that passes the recorded version actually used.
