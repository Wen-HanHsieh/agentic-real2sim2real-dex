Criteria text

Plug manipulation: my verification criteria, version 1

These are my own research criteria, not an official evaluation. They apply to the whole deployment task: two or three plugs, either kind, in any order, with varied right-side poses including touching and leaning. The robot has the specified right Wuji Hand 2.2 and floating wrist. An episode lasts 30 seconds. A placement or policy failure counts as a failure; it is not silently retried or removed.

Evidence and interpretation

The supplied 436-frame video is 14.53 seconds at 30 fps. Reference reconstruction and its validation/limitations are recorded before these criteria. The current reference directory is research/recon_final (record r 0009, reconstruction version 5); source and metric files are in analysis/, hand/, pose6d/, and interaction/. The objects are rigid Apple 20 W-like white and Mi 10 W-like black charger form factors; exact identity, mass distribution and hidden poses remain inferred priors. Both have fixed flat blades, a plastic body and a USB end opposite the blades.

The ordered demonstrated events are: approach palm-down; enclose a body with thumb and opposing finger; lift; turn blades up using finger motion together with wrist motion; carry left; lower the USB-end base; open and withdraw; repeat for the other plug. White contact is approximately frames 70–216 (2.33–7.20 s), black 252–384 (8.40–12.80 s), with about 4 frames boundary uncertainty. Lift/touchdown depths are partly occluded: support transitions are inferred around white 88–100 and 185–200; black 278–288 and 355–372. Those inferred instants are not exact timing targets.

Measured from the current shared reconstruction: center displacement approximately 218 mm white and 221 mm black, predominantly leftward; peak bottom clearances approximately 52 mm and 93 mm. The final supported blade direction is vertical. These metric values depend on assumed scale and camera; a sensitivity study under explicitly assumed focal/size/corner perturbations gives first-frame optical depths about 350–497 mm (5 th–95 th percentiles), not a calibrated confidence interval. Global metric uncertainty must not be mistaken for precise hand contact geometry.

Anatomical wrist axes are reconstructed from wrist/MCP joints: palm normal and forward toward middle MCP. Their robot counterparts are root+Y and root−Z. Approximate median palm angles from down, for approach/turn/lower-release, are white 9.5/26.3/43.6 degrees and black 39.3/67.9/52.1 degrees. Forward-axis elevation is about 8/5/14 degrees for white and 40/48/11 degrees for black. Net wrist changes over pickup are approximately 37 degrees white and 48 degrees black; object-relative-to-wrist endpoint changes are approximately 83 degrees and 110 degrees before resolving the 180 degree blade-axis gauge. These are estimated, not directly measured orientations under occlusion. Standard opposed-grasp support is about 0.986 white/0.93 black on jointly supported frames; the camera and hand-fit limitations remain part of the evidence.

Quantities and thresholds

Use the true simulated robot/object states and collision contacts for verification, not rewards or policy stage labels. Only deployable observations may enter the actor. Let blade-up tilt be acos of the object's+Z dotted with world+Z. A plug's side is its body centerY as specified in TASK_CONFIG, not its blade tip. Let an object be held when it contacts the hand and has no table/floor/other-object contact. Sustained intervals allow gaps of at most 10 ms from contact discretization; otherwise the interval restarts.

For every object, collect these events in order:

  1. Grasp. Thumb and at least one of index/middle/ring/little contact its body during a continuous 100 ms enclosure interval. Across the subsequent held transport, thumb and another finger must both contact for at least 60% of the held samples. The demonstration supports near-continuous opposition;60% allows rolling contact, different link/pad anatomy and brief finger exchanges. Contacts confined to blades do not constitute the demonstrated body grasp.
  2. Lift. While touching the hand, the object's lowest solid point is at least 20 mm above the tabletop for at least 200 ms, and the object has no environment/other-object contact during that interval. The demonstrated peaks are 52/93 mm. Twenty millimeters clears a 16 mm blade projection with margin; the dwell distinguishes a controlled pickup from a collision bounce. No more than one object may be held for over 50 ms at once; brief clutter jostling is allowed, simultaneous carrying is not.
  3. Turn with the demonstrated wrist/finger relationship. For an initially nonupright plug (initial blade tilt>30 degrees), it reaches blade tilt<=20 degrees while held before final lowering. During its grasp-to-touchdown interval the object changes orientation relative to the wrist by at least 25 degrees, measured as the shortest rotation modulo the identical-blade 180 degree symmetry. Net wrist rotation lies between 10 and 85 degrees. The reference changes are roughly 83/110 degrees relative and 37/48 degrees wrist; broad tolerances account for inferred orientation, different hand proportions and adapted starting faces. This excludes pure whole-wrist carriage and stationary-wrist finger-only manipulation. An already upright plug still must be picked and carried, but needs no artificial turn.
  4. Carry, lower and release. It crosses toY>0.15 while held, and is then set on its USB-end base on the table with blades up, followed by release. A pickup from within the border does not need an arbitrary fixed 25 cm carry; the demonstration's roughly 22 cm displacement is adapted to the starting location. Its successful grasp/lift/turn must precede placement. A push, throw, floor landing or continued hand prop does not satisfy this event.

The wrist must remain recognizably in the demonstrated family of poses for each object's events. During approach/enclosure, median palm angle from down<=60 degrees and median forward-axis elevation lies within[-25,65]degrees. During held turning/carrying, median palm angle<=85 degrees and no palm inversion beyond 100 degrees lasts over 100 ms. During lowering/release, median palm angle<=70 degrees and median forward elevation is within[-20,40]degrees. Measured phase medians appear above; the 20–25 degree pose tolerances cover camera/up-direction sensitivity (about 7 degrees under the assumed perturbations) plus human/robot palm geometry and adaptation. The forward axis must point generally toward the selected object: median angle between it and object-minus-wrist<=60 degrees during enclosure. The reference approaches the object from behind its fingers;60 degrees admits the thumb-side pinch aperture. These criteria use axes and phase, not the video’s fixed world positions.

Final goal and episode success

Throughout the last 2 seconds, every one of the initialOobjects must have centerY>0.15, blade tilt<=15 degrees, its base touching the table, no hand or other-object support/contact, linear speed<=0.01 m/s and angular speed<=0.1 rad/s. Its lowest solid point must remain within[-1,3]mm of the table. All objects must remain on the given table. None may be dropped, omitted or credited because it is out of view.

The video shows approximately 1.7 seconds of the black plug standing after release and much longer for white; the 2 second check extends this slightly to test continued self-support. Fifteen degrees is below the nominal static tipping angles (roughly 27 degrees black,33 degrees white for centered COMs), with allowance for unknown COM and reconstruction tilt. The support tolerance represents contact compliance/numerics, not a relaxed side boundary. The velocity limits allow minor settling, not transport. No required physical threshold is relaxed based on a candidate's results.

Every controller tick must satisfy the hardware position allowance, speed and applied-effort limits from hardware/SPECS and RIGHT_HAND; malformed/nonfinite actions are failures. Wrist clipping follows the public controller and workspace. Any finger hardware violation makes episode success false, irrespective of task events.

Success means all objects satisfy every applicable event and wrist condition in order, sequentially, and the final goal holds without a hardware violation. The ordered suite stages are first_grasp, first_lift, first_turn, first_place, all_placed; success is the final stable whole-task result. A stage reports actual verified events, not the policy's internal state. first_place already includes the first object's complete demonstrated-style prefix; all_placed requires every object's prefix. Also report placed count, per-object events, wrist metrics and first failure.

Starts, pass rate and limits of the claim

The frozen task suite will use at least 32 fixed dev seeds and 32 fresh final episodes. Starts include both counts (balanced), both kinds, randomized lying faces/yaw, separated and touching/leaning groups, and the full usable right-side tabletop (centers inset only enough to keep initial solids on the table). Randomize geometry and physics around the sourced priors; state exact distributions in the simulation/suite record. Starts that expose an unreachable hardware/workspace situation remain failures and are identified; they are not removed after evaluation.

My pass rate is 90% episode success, with zero hardware-violation episodes. For three objects that corresponds to about 96.5% per-object reliability if independent, a demanding whole-sequence research threshold. Report the gate's observed rate and 95%Wilson interval; passing a finite suite does not establish universal coverage, sim-to-real validity or production reliability. Choose the candidate on dev gates only. The final verdict names its single fresh-seed final gate. Target rounds are judged trial by trial against the same goal/skill criteria, using state/contact logs and video; unavailable evidence is uncertainty, not automatic success.

Open choices: any pickup order and final spacing are allowed, and the hand may start at a chosen safe pose. Identical-blade yaw sign is a gauge, not a task goal. No cables are present. Object count is observed, and all actual objects count. Human finger angles are not imposed joint-for-joint on a different mechanism; contact changes, in-hand turning and anatomical wrist axes express the skill. Touching/leaning starts are included, while final independent standing follows the visible separated outcome. Exact hidden depths and manufacturer-specific dynamics remain limitations of the reconstruction and are randomized rather than presented as known facts.
