Version 2 changes and evidence

Gate r0009 has 0/64 success, 37 hardware violations and a reproducibility failure. Its renderer could not find scene.mjb, so it provided no montages. I did not inspect nonexistent images. I locally reproduced its selected seeds 1873873406 and 1054510092 with the same v1 controller and the standard renderer; these are explicitly my reruns, not the original gate trajectories. In the first rerun at 10–16 s the plug hangs below a nearly flat palm, and at 22 s it is released without stable placement. In the second, it pivots in the grip between 6 and 16 s and lies unsupported by the hand at 22 s. See research/r0009_reproduction/montage_*.jpg and videos. The gate's measured passive rotation and failed turn support this visual diagnosis.

The original wrist upper bounds alone were too weak to represent the instruction's emphasis on wrist pose. Strengthen them, never relax the goal:

  • During the qualifying active turn, the palm's median normal tilt from down must be at least 5 degrees and at most 75 degrees, and its rotation from the start of that turn must be at least 5 degrees and at most 60. The measured human turns have 26/64 degree median tilt and 27/17 degree wrist rotation. Five degrees is a deliberately broad lower bound after anatomical retargeting and the reconstruction's substantial correlated orientation uncertainty; it excludes an entirely rigid, fixed wrist.
  • During the qualifying airborne carry, palm-normal tilt from down must be in [20,75] degrees for at least 0.10 s. Human carry medians are 41/54 degrees with upper quantiles 42/55. A 20-degree lower bound allows about 20 degrees of retargeting and monocular uncertainty from the smaller measured tilt, while excluding the flat-wrist carry seen in the rerun.
  • The 0.40 rad finger excursion in the turn must come from fingers that actually contact the plug. At least 75% of its accumulated relative angular path, measured in consecutive ~0.1 s windows of the qualifying turn, must occur while contacting-finger joint-speed norm is at least 0.30 rad/s. Joints of fingers not touching at both ends of a window do not count. This prevents unrelated finger motion from satisfying the active-turn proxy. The measured human joint speed is about 1.0–1.2 rad/s in its turn; the 0.30 threshold follows the standard measurement's conservative definition. The 75% requirement interprets the instruction that most turning is active and complements the unchanged 20-degree total passive-rotation bound. It is a kinematic indicator, not a force- causality proof: moving contacting fingers can still permit slipping.

All original thresholds, the exact given success-condition block, the full start distribution, 60 s episode, no-stop/hardware rules and 0.80 pass rate remain. Stages after a failed earlier stage are now reported false explicitly.
