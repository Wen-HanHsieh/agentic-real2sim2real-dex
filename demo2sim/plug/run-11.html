Verification criteria — version 2

These are my simulation criteria, not an official evaluation. They apply to every plug present, whether there are two or three, regardless of kind, lying direction, touching or leaning neighbours. A sampled success rate does not prove universal success.

Evidence and uncertainty

Sources: TASK.md; the 436-frame video; reconstruction v7 (r0014; table-consistent post-Stage-1 repair), especially analysis/quantities.json, analysis/calibration_sensitivity.json and the 16-event decomposition. White contact is approximately frames 78–220 (4.77 s), black 270–386 (3.90 s), with 3–6 frames of boundary uncertainty. Estimated left displacement is 0.214/0.222 m, peak bottom clearance 16–19/10–12 mm across focal sensitivity fits, object turn about 98/93 degrees, anatomical wrist turn about 31/37 degrees, and object blade-axis turn relative to the wrist about 72/83 degrees. The palm remains downward-facing but tilts more for black (up to about 68 degrees from downward).

Stage-1 reconstruction passed the supplied checks but an independent hand/table check found up to 78.9 mm penetration. The recorded post-Stage-1 revision adds full-vertex table barriers and depth ordering to the MANO fit: all supplied checks pass and no supported hand vertex is below the table. The revised hand scale is 1.044. Camera/scale uncertainty remains substantial; absolute position uncertainty is conservatively about 75 mm, while relative transport/rotation is better constrained. The revision changes the measured wrist turn to approximately 31/37 degrees and relative object orientation turn to 77/92 degrees. The thresholds below are unchanged. Absolute human wrist positions are still only a retargeting seed: the mapped robot mount minimum is 5.954 mm, while the hardware minimum is 30 mm, so at least 24.046 mm upward adaptation is necessary. We therefore imitate relative motion and phase-dependent orientation, then prove contacts dynamically with the robot.

Goal

At the end of the full 30 s episode, for each plug:

  • Housing centre has world y > +0.15 m (the configured left side), and its entire collision geometry remains on the tabletop footprint.
  • The angle between its local blade axis +Z and world +Z is at most 10 degrees. The video shows approximately vertical blades; 10 degrees allows reconstruction/geometry error while still requiring a stable standing configuration.
  • Its housing bottom, rather than blade tips or another plug, is supported by the table. Bottom clearance is within 3 mm of the surface, table contact is present, and there is no hand or object support. The 3 mm tolerance covers soft contact and numerical penetration, not floating placement.
  • It is free of the hand and has remained at rest for at least the final 1 s, with speed < 0.01 m/s and angular speed < 0.10 rad/s throughout that second. The video gives about 7.17 s white and 1.63 s black of final rest; 1 s is the common observable hold and detects tipping after release.

Every plug must satisfy these simultaneously, including plugs disturbed later. No plug is lost, swept off the table or left on the right.

Skill and ordered subgoals

Each plug must pass these in order; objects may be selected in any order, one at a time.

  1. Pickup: capture the housing with thumb and at least one other finger, separate it from table/other objects, and hold an opposed grip continuously for at least 0.5 s. During that hold its lowest geometry is at least 5 mm above the table. The demonstrated clearances are 10–19 mm; 5 mm accommodates relative depth uncertainty while excluding a slide. The 0.5 s interval excludes incidental/bouncing contacts and is much shorter than the demonstrated multi-second grasp. No two objects are held simultaneously.
  2. Active reorientation: let beta be the initial blade-axis angle from upward. If beta >= 30 degrees, turn the blade axis toward upward while in an opposed grip, with blade-axis rotation relative to the wrist at least 0.5 beta. The reference has roughly 72/83 degrees of relative blade-axis turn for roughly 90-degree starts; one half allows pose/adaptation uncertainty and still excludes a wrist-only turn. For nearly upright starts, no unnecessary turn is required. Relative motion accumulated while the finger joints are nearly still must be <= max(10 degrees, 0.35 times total relative rotation); this is an explicit anti-slip assumption, using meas-2's 0.30 rad/s joint-speed and 0.10 s window convention.
  3. Carry and lower: carry the plug to the left in a supported grasp, lower its bottom onto the table, then release. Table support precedes the final hand-contact break. No throwing, deliberate drop, table-assisted flipping or pushing substitutes for the demonstrated manipulation. A free-flight interval longer than 0.10 s fails. Already placed plugs must remain stable or be repaired before completion.
  4. Wrist/fingers: preserve the demonstrated phase-dependent wrist style. The palm normal stays within 75 degrees of downward for at least 95% of opposed handling time. This covers the observed maximum about 68 degrees plus gravity/pose uncertainty. After allowing one constant yaw adaptation per object and monotone phase alignment, at least 90% of handling samples must be within 35 degrees of the kind's reference wrist orientation. The 35-degree tolerance covers morphology and uncertain occluded pose, not an extra 90-degree wrist flip. During a normal lying start (beta <= 120 degrees), wrist rotation in the turning grip must be <= 65 degrees (observed 31/37 plus 26-degree tolerance). More inverted starts permit at most max(65 degrees, 0.5 beta + 20 degrees), still requiring active finger reorientation. Finger motion must accompany the turn; a fixed-finger slip fails the preceding test.
  5. Independent standing: meet all final-goal predicates, after release and withdrawal, before the next pickup is considered complete.

The evaluator reports cumulative episode stages pickup, reorient, placed, complete: first successful pickup; first successful active/appropriate reorientation; first independently standing placement; all objects satisfying every goal and skill criterion. It also reports per-object event times and first failures. An episode succeeds only when all criteria, in order, and every hardware rule hold.

Measurements, suites and pass rates

Standard meas-2 logs contain all object/wrist states, finger joints and contact flags. Guards use held_s, opposed_s, opposed_rel_rotation_deg, passive_rel_rotation_deg, opposed_wrist_rotation_deg, final_y_m, final_axis_angle_deg (+Z against +Z), rest_at_end_s, final speeds and end contacts. Exact geometry support, ordering, continuous intervals, adaptive blade-axis rotation, wrist-reference similarity and absence of throwing are additionally computed from these logs and the model. The frozen evaluator's adaptive checks are necessary: a single unconditional relative-rotation threshold would incorrectly demand turning an already upright start.

The task suite will use 64 fixed development starts (32 two-plug and 32 three-plug), with both kinds, all lying yaw/roll directions, separated/touching/leaning layouts, broad table locations and documented physical-parameter variation. Starts near workspace limits are not silently omitted; unreachable or unhandled starts count as failures. A final gate uses 64 fresh starts. The required success rate is 80% with zero hardware-violation episodes. This is a demanding practical robustness threshold under uncertain geometry/clutter within the available research budget; every failure and its uncertainty remains visible.

Primitives to prove before whole-task PPO are opposed_pickup, active_reorientation, and supported_release. Each feasibility suite needs at least 50% success on 32 fixed jittered starts and zero hardware violations, proving that a real contact motion exists in the model, not that deployment is solved. Separately trained skills need their own suites and at least 85% success before being frozen in the library; their starts must come from the preceding incumbent's actual outcomes.

All finger position, speed and applied-torque rules apply at every physics step. Hand/table or hand/object penetration deeper than 3 mm fails my additional physical check. The episode remains 30 s / 15000 policy ticks at 500 Hz; completing early does not end evaluation. Target trials will be judged individually by the same goal/skill definitions and replayed before any repair.

Open assumptions

The exact chargers, internal mass distributions, surface friction and compliance are unknown priors. The objects are treated as rigid with fixed blades and no moving mechanism. Constant yaw adaptation and phase time alignment are my interpretation of adapting the demonstrated motion to arbitrary lying poses. An already upright plug on the right still needs pickup/carry/placement, but not an artificial extra turn. The current reference is table-consistent, but anatomical-to-robot morphology and monocular depth remain uncertain. No policy has yet been judged by these criteria.
