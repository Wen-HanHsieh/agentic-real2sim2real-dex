Agent verification criteria v1: unsupported in-hand plug turn

These are my own simulation criteria, not an official evaluation. They apply to one plug already held in the Wuji right hand. Pickup, transport across the desk and placement are excluded by TASK.md.

Evidence and interpretation

Reconstruction version3 (r0011) is the reference. The clear black turn is frames314–326, 10.467–10.867s: fitted object rotation relative to the hand76.3°, about starting-object axis(0.9584,0.2750,-0.0770), with20.6° wrist rotation. The thumb pushes/rolls the lower housing, index contact changes above it and middle supports. The white interval100–120, 3.333–4.000s, changes blade direction about73.3° relative to the hand with12.2° wrist rotation; its full111.2° geodesic includes uncertain axial spin. Early white base support is visually unresolved. The black interval supplies the clearly unsupported primitive; deployment prohibits external support in either case.

Estimated fingertip movements are on the order of20–45mm. Fitted hand variants give black relative turns74.8–77.1° while holding the final object poses fixed. Nominal object orientation uncertainty is20° on the selected symmetry branch; absolute translation uncertainty40mm reflects monocular scale. These are fitted, prior-dependent estimates, not motion-capture truth. Sources, limitations and reference logs are in research/reconstruction_v1/.

Deployment and evaluation family

The simulation record will specify the chosen stable wrist/finger poses and nominal grasp transforms for each known object_kind. Each episode starts at rest with hand support only. Reset may physically settle a candidate placement into its existing grasp; this must not perform the task's turn or use an attachment or external force. The frozen starts must pass the gate's1-second hold test on at least95% of64 fresh seeds.

Evaluate both kinds, selected deterministically from the seed with approximately equal probability. Cover translations up to±5mm along all grasp-frame axes and orientation perturbations up to±15° around the nominal grasp, with initial blade elevation angle75–105° from world-up. This is the chosen deployment start family, not a claim about every possible grasp or arbitrary orientation in the hand. Record actual settled starts and any unreachable requested placements. Do not silently shrink the family to pass.

Version2 clarification of already-held starts. The bounds above define a proposal envelope. A proposal is eligible only when physical preparation leaves it supported by the hand, without environment contact, with the blades still75–105° from up and orientation change at most20° during settling. Proposals that fall or pivot into an upright/inverted grasp are recorded and resampled within the same bounds; no task-turn controller or task-success check is used for selection. Report acceptance rate and the actual accepted pose/physics distributions, including any poorly covered region. This conditions the distribution on TASK.md's already-held deployment, rather than treating a falling object as an initial grasp. The independent gate still tests95% stability on fresh seeds. Goal, motion, pass-rate and hardware thresholds are unchanged.

The robustness family includes housing scale0.90–1.10 for white and0.85–1.15 for black, mass0.038–0.083kg white/0.020–0.070kg black, COM offsets up to3mm, sliding friction0.4–1.2, and low restitution0–0.1. These are explicit uncertainty assumptions because the exact objects and materials are unknown. The evaluator freezes and records its distributions; implementation constraints or justified revisions must be recorded before relying on different ranges. Finger encoder noise is exactly the hardware specification. No contact, force, torque or physical-parameter input is available to the reported policy.

  1. secured. During the initial0.5s the object remains in the opposed hand grasp and is physically supported, without external contact, a drop or a large placement transient. Entry-link stabilization may last1.2s in its own suite. Its final0.2s object speed must be below0.05m/s and angular speed below0.5rad/s, the same physical-rest scale as the gate's start check. Thumb plus at least one other finger must support it; finger/palm proximity alone is insufficient.
  2. finger_turn. Before the final hold, the object rotates at least60° relative to the wrist within one continuously opposed grip interval. This is below the fitted76.3° by16.3° to allow orientation uncertainty and morphological retargeting while requiring a substantial majority of the demonstrated turn. The wrist rotates at most25° from its start, allowing the observed20.6° plus about4° fit sensitivity. Passive relative rotation is at most15° using the standard0.30rad/s joint-speed/window test. Require the thumb group and at least one of index/middle to each change joint configuration by a norm of at least0.15rad during the turning interval, clearly above0.002rad encoder noise. Unrelated finger wiggling alone does not satisfy this extra check. The intended net turn follows the demonstrated predominantly+X pitch; the episode's orientation goal and videos must show the same side-to-up finger roll, with any necessary morphological deviation recorded.
  3. steady. The blades' object-frame+Z axis stays within10° of world+Z for the entire final1.0s, with the object still held in an opposed grasp. Over that second, object linear speed is at most0.01m/s and angular speed at most0.10rad/s. Ten degrees gives a clear upright goal (dot product>=0.9848), and1s distinguishes a maintained hold from briefly crossing upright. These are engineering tolerances, not measurements of a stationary final hand hold: the demonstration proceeds into carrying/placement after the turn. Any yaw about the upward blade direction is acceptable. Use a sufficiently dense set of yaw-equivalent goal quaternions for the standard relative-goal check.

Throughout an episode the object must touch no environment or other object, remain within0.18m of the wrist, and remain hand-supported. No support gap longer than0.04s is allowed, and hand-supported duration must be at least episode_duration−0.05s; the small sampling allowance is not permission for a ballistic flick. An object flight cannot contribute to the opposed-grip rotation. All finger position/speed/applied-torque hardware rules apply at every1kHz tick, with zero episodes containing hardware violations permitted in a passing gate. Initial geometry must be free of material interpenetration; report deepest contact penetration in rollouts.

An8s whole-task horizon allows stabilization, a slower robot finger roll and the final1s hold. The source turns take0.4–0.67s; slower execution is an explicit morphology/control-robustness deviation because TASK.md specifies the motion and goal, not a deadline. Primitive suites may have shorter horizons, declared before registration.

Revision 3: enforce demonstrated wrist pose and blade-axis reorientation

This revision tightens fidelity after auditing the full reconstructed palm/object transforms. Earlier versions and their results keep their original labels. No goal, stability, support, hardware, pass-rate or original active-motion threshold is relaxed.

At the initial frame, gravity expressed in the robot wrist must lie within20degrees of the gravity vector in the corresponding reconstructed palm frame. The robot convention is +Y palmar and -Z finger-forward, as used by analysis/motion_metrics.json's robot_wrist_reference_rotation. World yaw remains free because the goal and scene are yaw invariant while the object is unsupported externally. The reference downward-gravity vectors in that wrist frame are white[-0.187543,0.982079,0.018686] and black[-0.5858,0.3782,0.7170] (the evaluator uses the full-precision recorded matrices). The representative palmar-normal tilts are10.86degrees white and67.78degrees black. The20degree tolerance is the explicitly assumed conditional orientation uncertainty of reconstructionv3 and allows anatomical retargeting; it is not a posterior accuracy guarantee.

The blades must additionally turn by at least60degrees relative to the wrist within one continuous opposed interval. Compute unit blade vectors b(t)=Rw(t)^T Ro(t)[0,0,1]. For each continuous opposed interval[a,b], compute max_t acos(b(a) dot b(t)); take the maximum across intervals. This uses the blade-direction changes measured in the reference:73.35degrees white and76.02degrees black. A13–16degree margin allows tracking uncertainty and robot morphology while retaining the main demonstrated turn. This condition prevents extra axial spin from substituting for the demonstrated blade-direction change. The existing full relative rotation>=60degrees, passive<=15degrees, thumb/index-or-middle motion>=.15rad and wrist excursion<=25degrees also remain required.

The nominal held orientations and hand configuration will be retargeted from the recorded wrist/object transforms, rather than the original canonical object-in-wrist identity. Object pose proposals remain±5mm and±15degrees, with fixed physical draws, hand-only free preparation, accepted initial world blade angle75–105degrees and the recorded physical support checks. Any nominal wrist adjustment must satisfy the new gravity-in-wrist20degree bound. Record the exact chosen hand/object poses in the next simulation version. White contact depth is less certain than black; contact-region assumptions and robot deviations remain explicit.
