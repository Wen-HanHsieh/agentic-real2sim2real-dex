Own verification criteria, version3

These are my simulation claims and tests, not an official evaluation. Earlier policies were evaluated under recorded versions1/2; this revision is recorded before judging a wrist-assisted learned policy. The goal applies to either plug and any physically held placement: use active fingers to bring the blades up relative to gravity, keep the plug in the hand, and hold it steady. Finite tests establish performance only on their stated distributions.

Evidence and interpretation

TASK.md excludes pickup, transport and placement. It requires an already-held plug, finger-driven turning relative to a wrist posed like the demonstration, continuing holding, and no other object touching the plug. The reconstruction version used by the simulation supplies meshes, frames and uncertain motion; it is not a physical feasibility proof.

Reference windows are white frames100–140 (3.33–4.67s), black285–325 (9.50–10.83s), each1.33s. Recorded version4 estimates approximately72°/62° net rotation relative to the hand, mainly about the negative object-width axis (+/-X is indistinguishable under identical-blade symmetry). Wrist changes are about26° each. Upright endpoint errors are about5°/15°; partial occlusion, focal/depth and anatomical fits leave approximately30° assigned rotational uncertainty. The final recorded reference version and its exact measurements supersede these rounded values where a later support correction changes them.

Visible thumb opposition and changing index/middle curvature are essential evidence. Fitted human bend excursions are roughly24–32° for the thumb and27–28° for the index's most changing bend. Human and robot motor coordinates differ. The carried upright holds last approximately1.2–1.4s; this run retains a1s steady hold and excludes carrying.

Ordered stages

  1. hold_start: for at least0.20s, the already-held plug is supported by the thumb and at least one of index/middle/ring/pinky. A deployment reset may not weld, attach, constrain or externally support the plug. Object and hand contacts must come from physics. This short period distinguishes a real initial grasp from a kinematic placement that immediately drops.
  2. turn: within4s, blades enter the upward cone (object +Z within15° of world +Z). During one continuous opposed grip the object must rotate relative to the wrist by at least45° on the registered lateral starts, and by at least the initial blade tilt minus15° minus the wrist’s actual start-to-entry rotation for that episode. The45° minimum always applies. This is a necessary bound from rotation composition; the world-up condition separately judges whether the combined motion actually reaches the goal. Its net rotation axis in its initial object frame must be within45° of +/-X; this contains both reconstructed axes plus their uncertainty, while rejecting unrelated axial spins. The thumb and at least one contacting opposing finger must each show at least0.15rad peak-to-peak change in a motor joint before reaching the goal. This is smaller than the fitted human bend changes because anatomy differs, yet far above encoder noise. At most20° of opposed relative rotation may occur while joints are nearly still (the standard passive-rotation measure). Maximum wrist rotation from its chosen starting stance is35°: approximately26° observed plus9° retargeting/estimate tolerance. The robot may hold its wrist fixed.
  3. steady: hold the goal through the final1s of a5s episode. Throughout that second: blades remain within15° of up, the plug remains in opposed hand contact, object speed is at most0.02m/s and angular speed at most0.25rad/s. These explicit engineering tolerances define “steady” (they are not measured transport speeds). The plug touches neither table/floor nor any support or other object at any time; the hand must not brace on the environment. Hardware rules must hold at every controller tick. The object's final orientation relative to the wrist must be within25° of an upright goal, allowing equivalent yaw goals around its blade axis.

Success means all three stages in order, with no drop, external support, hand bracing or hardware violation. Evaluation must report each stage and every hardware failure, including failures after an earlier stage succeeds. All actions in a reported learned policy must come from a PPO-trained deployable network; a feasibility controller is reported separately.

Standard gate measurements

hold_start: opposed_s >= .2, held_s >= .2, plus the evaluator's actual initial interval check.

turn: opposed_rel_rotation_deg >= 45, passive_rel_rotation_deg <= 20, wrist.max_rotation_deg <= 35; the evaluator additionally checks episode-dependent required turn, contacting-finger motion, axis and first goal entry. Full state logs preserve evidence for independent gate measurement.

steady: final_axis_angle_deg <= 15 (object_axis[0,0,1], world_axis[0,0,1]), held_s >= 1, touching_hand_at_end >= 1, environment_contact_s <= 0, last_second_max_speed_m_s <= .02, last_second_max_angular_speed_rad_s <= .25, goal_error_deg <= 25, wrist.max_rotation_deg <= 35. The evaluator additionally checks all final-second samples, opposition and hand/environment contacts. The primitive gate also adds its own opposed-rotation, passive-rotation and goal predicates from the measured reference angle.

Starts, uncertainty and pass rates

The holding joint pose and exact placement generator must be physically established and documented in the training-simulation record before freezing suites. The intended deployment family spans both kinds equally, lateral blade tilts65–115°, roll/yaw perturbations up to20°, placement translations up to8mm, and perturbed hand pose/velocities within the grasp. No start may be made artificially easy by beginning upright, adding support, or attaching the object. Any necessary bounds change is recorded with physical evidence and a new suite, without silently changing the goal or thresholds.

Training and evaluation must vary mass, inertia/COM, friction, body dimensions and starting placement around the uncertain public priors. The exact distributions are stated in the simulation/suites; the actor may not observe these parameters or contacts. Out-of-family stress tests are reported separately and cannot be relabeled as deployment coverage. A finite random-start pass rate is not proof over every possible placement or real plug.

The task and deployable skill require at least0.80 success with zero episodes violating hardware rules; this is a deliberately demanding empirical reliability threshold, not a guarantee. Use at least64 fixed dev seeds and128 fresh final episodes, balanced between kinds where the seed generator permits. Report Wilson intervals and per-kind results. A narrow feasibility suite may require0.25 to prove that a physical finger motion exists; that is never called a mastered skill or a task pass. The skill suite must reach0.80 before whole-task gates during the first60% of the budget.

Ambiguities and deviations

The exact brands, physical parameters, hidden contact points and camera scale are unknown. Object +Z is defined by blades; yaw about it is irrelevant to the goal. A common palm-down wrist stance matching the white example is chosen for both plugs; the black pickup's more tilted stance is not required. A5s robot episode allows a slower3–4s turn and1s settling/holding because rigid robot joints and pads differ from human skin; it does not waive active finger turning. The task goal takes priority over a reconstructed endpoint's residual tilt. Any later deviation, primitive redesign or criteria revision must be recorded with its reason before use.

Revision2: active commands as well as joint motion

Before any policy evaluation, tighten the active-finger test: both the thumb and one contacting opposing finger must have at least0.15rad peak-to-peak position-target change before first goal entry, in addition to the same requirement on actual joint positions. Log joint_target alongside standard states. Direct-drive joints can back-drive under gravity while commands stay fixed; actual movement alone does not prove deliberate finger actuation. All goal, hardware, rate and deployment requirements remain unchanged.

Revision3: count wrist and finger contributions in their own frames

The demonstration has roughly62–72° relative finger-driven rotation and25–26° wrist rotation. Version2 allowed wrist motion but its episode-dependent bound implicitly required fingers alone to supply the entire world correction. Before enabling learned wrist commands, correct that bound to max(45°, initial blade tilt−15°−actual wrist rotation at first goal entry). It is identical to version2 for a fixed wrist. This follows the rotation-distance triangle inequality, not an assumption that wrist and finger axes coincide. The wrist still stays within35°, finger motion/commands remain active, passive relative motion stays<=20°, and the object must actually be upright and steadily unsupported. A wrist-only solution cannot pass.

The upright goal is fixed in the world:24 equivalent yaw orientations with +Z up. For the standard final relative-goal measurement, express those fixed world goals in the final wrist frame. This coordinate conversion does not change the world goal; it avoids incorrectly treating a world-up goal as fixed to a moving wrist. All stage ordering, 4s first-entry deadline, 5s episode, final1s tolerances, starts, pass rates and hardware rules remain unchanged.
