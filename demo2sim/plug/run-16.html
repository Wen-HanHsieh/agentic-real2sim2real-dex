Criteria text

My verification criteria: complete plug clearing, version 2

These are my own simulation claims, not an official evaluation. They apply to every plug in an episode and to either geometry kind, in any mixture and with two or three objects. The policy must pick objects that start on the right, turn them with its fingers, carry them left, put them down and release them. A held-object turning result alone does not meet this task.

Evidence and uncertainty

The original video has 436 frames at 30 Hz. Reconstruction versions 1 and 2 were recorded before these criteria; the refined visible-feature reference and its measurements are in research/recon_v3/. Video inspection, not an inherited success claim, supplies the following ordered plan:

The refined primary turns estimate approximately 62.5 degrees relative to the palm for white and 107.0 degrees for black; wrist changes are approximately 15.3 and 34.0 degrees. Thumb/index fingertip excursions relative to the palm are about 16/14 mm and 35/36 mm. The whole grasp/carry/place wrist changes reach about 46/51 degrees; wrist inclination changes reach about 37/45 degrees. Whole-task phase values and poses are saved, not inferred from a policy's results.

The metric reference estimates about 19 cm of transfer, while demo/README describes about 25 cm. Scale, focal length, support orientation and similar-object dimensions remain coupled. Object rotation uncertainty is at least 30 degrees; some white depth corrections are 62 mm. White frames 108–117 and 73 black frames remain invalid. These limitations prevent precise human joint-angle or contact-force ground truth. They do not waive the deployed goal or active finger manipulation.

Per-object stages, in order

  1. Pick and lift. The object starts supported on the right, body center y <= 0.15 m. It rises at least 0.03 m above its settled start height and spends at least 0.25 s in an opposed grip clear of the environment and other plugs. Its lowest collision surface reaches at least 0.015 m above the table. The reconstructed peak rises are several centimeters; 3 cm/1.5 cm distinguish lifting from tipping or dragging while allowing monocular height uncertainty and a different robot finger size. Standard measures: max_rise_m, opposed_s, held_s; exact surface clearance is also checked from the model.
  1. Turn with the fingers. Within one continuous opposed interval, the casing rotates at least 47 degrees relative to the wrist, then its blade axis is within 15 degrees of world +Z while still held. Forty-seven degrees is the rounded 75% of the conservative visible white turn (62.5 degrees), not a threshold selected from policy scores. Passive relative rotation in still-joint 0.1 s windows is at most 15 degrees. At least one actually contacting thumb, index or middle finger must change actual joint configuration by a norm of 0.12 rad and move its pad by 4 mm relative to the wrist during this turn. The 4 mm threshold is below the smallest observed contacting thumb/index excursion (~14 mm); 0.12 rad is an explicit robot articulation assumption (~7 degrees), not a measured human encoder value. Contacts with table, floor, or another object during the turn are disallowed. Standard measures: opposed_rel_rotation_deg, passive_rel_rotation_deg, opposed_s, goal_error_deg; the evaluator checks final blade direction at the turn event. Starts that happen to be blades up still require an active relative turn, which may be about the blade axis. This is an intentionally strict skill check.
  1. Carry, place and release. Only one object is carried at once. After the turn, it remains held clear of other objects and the table until lowering for placement. Its blade axis stays within 30 degrees of up during transport (observed carry tilts extend to about 26 degrees, with uncertain endpoints). It ends with body center y > 0.15 m and blades within 15 degrees of up. The body bottom, rather than the blade tips, supports it. It touches the table, no longer touches the hand, and remains independently at rest for the final 1.0 s: speed <0.01 m/s and angular speed <0.10 rad/s throughout that second. It may not lean on another plug; there is no object–object contact in the final second. The video provides at least ~1.5 s of common final rest. Fifteen degrees allows small placement/model errors while excluding a plug left lying down; resting contact and speed checks are additionally required. Standard measures: final_y_m, final_axis_angle_deg (+Z object axis against world +Z), touching_environment_at_end, touching_hand_at_end, rest_at_end_s, last_second_max_speed_m_s, last_second_max_angular_speed_rad_s.

Wrist and physical execution

  • During the main turn the wrist changes by at most 45 degrees from turn onset. During the complete opposed manipulation/carry/place interval it changes by at most 60 degrees, with wrist inclination changing by at most 55 degrees. These limits come from measured 34/51/45 degree maxima plus about 10 degrees for retargeting and pose estimation. Wrist inclination compares the world-up vector expressed in the wrist, so object-dependent approach yaw is free.
  • Wrist attitude must stay within 30 degrees, on average, and 45 degrees at every held frame, of the demonstrated anatomical palm attitudes for that phase after one constant world-Z yaw alignment per object. Either observed white/black grasp variant may be used for either kind. Reference axes match Wuji: +X toward thumb, +Y palm normal, -Z along long fingers. This checks full palm attitude, not merely wrist translation; it excludes turning the hand upside down to substitute for the finger motion. Approach yaw and distance adapt to each start.
  • No object attachments, object actuation, teleportation, gravity changes or collision disabling can support the motion. The deepest hand–object collision penetration must be <=3 mm, an explicit compliant-pad modeling allowance. Table/floor and object–object contacts remain enabled.
  • Every physics tick respects hwspec-1.3 finger position, speed and applied torque limits. Actual joint velocities are never clipped to make an episode pass. Any violation makes the episode fail. Commands use the specified wrist/finger controllers. Actor observations are only the allowed noisy encoders, wrist and object states, object kinds, past commands, step and observation history.

Full-task success and test distribution

Full-task success means all objects complete these stages, in order for each object, within 60 simulated seconds, with the entire right side clear and every object independently standing on the left at the end. Every object, including the third one and objects initially touching/leaning, counts. No reduction to a single in-hand object is permitted. Failure or unsupported initial proposals are reported; deployment support is checked by a stationary hold-policy test.

Training and verification must include both object counts, any kind mixture, arbitrary horizontal placement across the table's right side (including edge cases), different settled faces and yaw, and touching/leaning clusters. Fixed development seeds are for debugging; gates draw fresh seeds. The final gate uses at least 128 episodes (planned 256), balanced by object count and separated versus clustered starts. Clustered trials mix touching and leaning. Kind mixtures, resting-face categories and physical variations are reported separately.

My pass rule is at least 80% full-task success with zero episodes containing a hardware violation, on the fresh final task gate. Eighty percent is a declared reliability target for this multi-object, multi-stage simulation, not an official score. Report the denominator and 95% Wilson interval, all stage counts and stratum results. Feasibility suites require at least 25%; deployable primitive skill suites require at least 80%. A feasibility controller is not the final learned policy. The repeated chain is pickup/lift -> finger turn -> place/release -> repeat for remaining objects; every transition uses actual predecessor ends.

Finite simulation tests do not establish success for every possible real start. Ideal object state access, uncertain object properties, contact approximations, perception delay, and real-hand transfer remain limitations even if this rule passes. Failure evidence and rejected hypotheses remain in the record.

Version 2 clarification, before whole-task evaluation

The goal, skill thresholds and80%/zero-HW pass rule are unchanged. The evaluator must distinguish actual tabletop support from the floor: with the blades up, the lowest collision surface must be within3mm of tabletopz0, in addition to positive environment contact and the existing independent1srest. This3mm numerical support tolerance matches the declared compliant-contact allowance; floor support at-.75m fails. No goal requires or rewards floor recovery.

Carry remains held and clear of environment/otherobjects after the successful turn until lowering begins on the left. Lowering onset is defined as the first left-side frame with lowest surface below3cm, a phase boundary consistent with the existing placement-height classification, not permission to throw the object. Blades remain within30deg until tabletop support, and final15deg/rest requirements remain. This closes an evaluation omission that could otherwise count a thrown plug or a floor placement. No whole-task policy has been gated under the omission.

For the repeat link, new pickup/turn evidence is required for initially uncompleted objects. The predecessor's already-cleared plug remains fully logged, must not be rehandled, and must finish independently upright on the tabletop. Contacts with it count as support of a manipulated object, so they cannot make a turn unsupported. Earlier criterion versions and primitive records are not relabelled.

The selected version4 black reference log measures110.3deg over308–335 (earlier version3 estimate107deg); the wrist change is33.9deg. This is within the stated pose uncertainty. The47deg pass threshold still derives from the conservative white62.5deg turn and is unchanged.

Version 3: necessary pickup handoff condition

The full-task goal and all numerical full-task thresholds remain exactly as version2. Global passive relative rotation must be <=15degrees for each object, including pickup, turn and carry. Repeated-chain tests showed that a pickup counted as ready could already exceed that entire budget, making its later completion impossible even when the isolated turn and placement passed.

The pickup primitive therefore gains a final stage, firm: all prior ready checks AND the selected object's cumulative passive relative rotation over the pickup episode <=15degrees, computed by the same recorded measure used in the whole-task judge. This is a necessary prefix condition, not a replacement for the global check. Earlier pickup records retain their original meaning. The primitive mastery threshold remains80% with zero hardware violations, and feasibility remains25%. Training may target the stricter8degree rotation path reserve, but8degrees is not an additional verification threshold. No unused finger movement may be introduced to hide rotation in this measure.

Reason: threefinger development had50ready/41within15degrees; the incumbent twofinger controller had52ready/22within15degrees, while repeated-chain failures accumulated excessive passive rotation. Reporting readiness alone would hide this important distinction. This revision is recorded before gates rely on the new firm stage. It does not retroactively relabel older records or claim the current library meets the strengthened primitive verification.

  1. Carry, place and release. Only one object is carried at once. After the turn, it remains held clear of other objects and the table until lowering for placement. Its blade axis stays within 30 degrees of up during transport (observed carry tilts extend to about 26 degrees, with uncertain endpoints). It ends with body center y > 0.15 m and blades within 15 degrees of up. The body bottom, rather than the blade tips, supports it. It touches the table, no longer touches the hand, and remains independently at rest for the final 1.0 s: speed <0.01 m/s and angular speed <0.10 rad/s throughout that second. It may not lean on another plug; there is no object–object contact in the final second. The video provides at least ~1.5 s of common final rest. Fifteen degrees allows small placement/model errors while excluding a plug left lying down; resting contact and speed checks are additionally required. Standard measures: final_y_m, final_axis_angle_deg (+Z object axis against world +Z), touching_environment_at_end, touching_hand_at_end, rest_at_end_s, last_second_max_speed_m_s, last_second_max_angular_speed_rad_s.

Wrist and physical execution

  • During the main turn the wrist changes by at most 45 degrees from turn onset. During the complete opposed manipulation/carry/place interval it changes by at most 60 degrees, with wrist inclination changing by at most 55 degrees. These limits come from measured 34/51/45 degree maxima plus about 10 degrees for retargeting and pose estimation. Wrist inclination compares the world-up vector expressed in the wrist, so object-dependent approach yaw is free.
  • Wrist attitude must stay within 30 degrees, on average, and 45 degrees at every held frame, of the demonstrated anatomical palm attitudes for that phase after one constant world-Z yaw alignment per object. Either observed white/black grasp variant may be used for either kind. Reference axes match Wuji: +X toward thumb, +Y palm normal, -Z along long fingers. This checks full palm attitude, not merely wrist translation; it excludes turning the hand upside down to substitute for the finger motion. Approach yaw and distance adapt to each start.
  • No object attachments, object actuation, teleportation, gravity changes or collision disabling can support the motion. The deepest hand–object collision penetration must be <=3 mm, an explicit compliant-pad modeling allowance. Table/floor and object–object contacts remain enabled.
  • Every physics tick respects hwspec-1.3 finger position, speed and applied torque limits. Actual joint velocities are never clipped to make an episode pass. Any violation makes the episode fail. Commands use the specified wrist/finger controllers. Actor observations are only the allowed noisy encoders, wrist and object states, object kinds, past commands, step and observation history.

Full-task success and test distribution

Full-task success means all objects complete these stages, in order for each object, within 60 simulated seconds, with the entire right side clear and every object independently standing on the left at the end. Every object, including the third one and objects initially touching/leaning, counts. No reduction to a single in-hand object is permitted. Failure or unsupported initial proposals are reported; deployment support is checked by a stationary hold-policy test.

Training and verification must include both object counts, any kind mixture, arbitrary horizontal placement across the table's right side (including edge cases), different settled faces and yaw, and touching/leaning clusters. Fixed development seeds are for debugging; gates draw fresh seeds. The final gate uses at least 128 episodes (planned 256), balanced by object count and separated versus clustered starts. Clustered trials mix touching and leaning. Kind mixtures, resting-face categories and physical variations are reported separately.

My pass rule is at least 80% full-task success with zero episodes containing a hardware violation, on the fresh final task gate. Eighty percent is a declared reliability target for this multi-object, multi-stage simulation, not an official score. Report the denominator and 95% Wilson interval, all stage counts and stratum results. Feasibility suites require at least 25%; deployable primitive skill suites require at least 80%. A feasibility controller is not the final learned policy. The repeated chain is pickup/lift -> finger turn -> place/release -> repeat for remaining objects; every transition uses actual predecessor ends.

Finite simulation tests do not establish success for every possible real start. Ideal object state access, uncertain object properties, contact approximations, perception delay, and real-hand transfer remain limitations even if this rule passes. Failure evidence and rejected hypotheses remain in the record.
