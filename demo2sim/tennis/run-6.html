Criteria text

Agent-defined verification criteria, version 1

These are my claims about my own simulation, not official evaluation criteria or a task score. They are fixed before building or judging a trained policy. Evidence is TASK.md, the native 30 fps video, recorded reconstruction version 5 (r0014), and its analysis/task_measurements.json, analysis/spin_summary.json, hand/uncertainty.json and analysis/wrist_rate_feasibility.json.

Reference evidence and conventions

The ordered task is: toss/catch; turn, toss/catch; brief turn, toss/catch; turn and hold. Native free-flight frames are 53–65, 164–177 and 201–215. Contacts begin at frames 66,178,216. Boundaries are uncertain by one frame. Reconstructed release/catch boundaries are 1.750/2.183 s, 5.450/5.917 s, 6.683/7.183 s; durations .433/.467/.500 s; apex rise from release .244/.281/.259 m; lateral displacements .016/.019/.014 m. Gravity is 9.81 m/s² by the flight model, not independent proof of calibration.

The two long turn intervals are frames 74–155 and 225–302; the short turn is 182–193. Most-complete texture diagnostics give wrist-relative angular paths about 6.8–7.4, .59–.74 and 6.6–7.2 rad, with gaps/noise. Two-frame diagnostics cover less motion and yield coverage-normalized estimates roughly 4.5–5.6, .54–.55 and 5.2–5.7 rad. These are partial estimates, not a complete recovered orientation. Supported-increment products for the most-complete diagnostics are approximately 2.9–3.0, .34–.49 and 2.5–2.7 rad, and omit unsupported intervals.

The final hold is frames 303–314, .400 s. Ball position relative to the reconstructed wrist changes by at most .00495 m, with mean relative speed .0223 m/s. Its median wrist-frame position is (.0131,.0402,−.0947) m and palm tilt is 32.4°. Overall wrist vertical span is .245 m; palm-tilt percentiles 5/50/95 are 23.0/32.4/40.4°. Sensitivity across six fits gives relative wrist-position deviation p95 .0194 m and wrist-orientation deviation p95 4.75°, max 6.19°. Fitted fingertips vary more and are not exact targets.

Use the reconstructed human wrist basis in sim/human_reference.npz: +X toward the thumb/index side, +Y outward palm normal, fingers along −Z. Compare it with the Wuji root axes specified in hardware/SPECS.md. Register only the chosen initial world translation and known initial yaw; do not fit an episode afterward to improve agreement. Hand morphology may add a documented constant wrist-to-ball placement offset, which cancels from relative wrist displacement. There is no wrist workspace box.

Episode and physical event definitions

An episode lasts 10.50 s after initialization. It begins with the ball already held, as in native frame 0; acquiring a ball from a table is not part of the demonstration. Initialization may use at most 1 s of real physics settling under the same hand controllers. No object attachment, weld, state reset or external ball force is allowed within the episode. Initialization failures count as failed trials.

A physical hand contact is a MuJoCo hand/ball contact with positive normal force greater than 1e−4 N. This tiny threshold rejects numerical zero forces, not a force level measured from video; the ball weighs about .566 N. A catch is the first post-flight hand contact followed by at least .080 s of retention, allowing no contact gap longer than .020 s. The shortest visible catch/stabilization interval is about .133 s; .080 s accounts for one-frame timing ambiguity. Retention requires the ball center within .180 m of the wrist and on the palm side (wrist-frame Y > 0). The human center is about .10 m from the wrist; the .180 m bound permits the larger robot fingers and fixed palm offset while excluding a distant ball. At least one hand contact must support the ball in 90% of retained samples. All distances and forces used for verification are simulator state, not actor observations.

A toss is a continuous interval without hand contact lasting at least .300 s, with an ascending ball, an apex and a descending ball before its catch. The peak distance from the ball surface to every hand collision shape must exceed .120 m. Reconstructed full-flight hand clearances have medians .148/.157/.199 m and minima above .023 m; .120 m peak clearance conservatively requires an unmistakable throw. Short regrasp contact gaps are not extra tosses. Floor or other environment contact by ball or hand is forbidden throughout settling and the episode.

Ordered task requirements

All requirements below must pass in order in the same uninterrupted episode.

  1. Three tosses and three catches, exactly. For tosses 1/2/3, release must be within .100 s of 1.750/5.450/6.683 s; catch within .120 s of 2.183/5.917/7.183 s. Durations must be within .067 s of .433/.467/.500 s. Endpoint ambiguity is up to two 30 fps frames; the slightly wider absolute catch window allows controller capture delay. No fourth flight of .300 s or longer is allowed.
  2. Flight motion. Apex rise above each release is within .060 m of .244/.281/.259 m. The tolerance covers roughly .035 m of half-frame release-height uncertainty at 2.2–2.35 m/s, scale uncertainty and retargeting/contact differences. Horizontal displacement from release to catch is at most .060 m (measured .014–.019 m, plus about one ball radius of capture/scale allowance). A quadratic fit to contact-free ball height, excluding 10 ms at each endpoint, must give downward acceleration within .5 m/s² of 9.81. This checks real free flight in the constructed simulation; no aerodynamic accuracy is claimed.
  3. First in-hand turn. After catch 1 and before release 2, within 2.35–5.28 s, the ball's cumulative rotation relative to the wrist must be at least 4.00 rad and its maximum geodesic departure from the orientation at the start of the turn at least π/2 rad. Count only retained samples; retention must hold for at least 90% of the turn interval and no gap may exceed .020 s.
  4. Brief second turn. After catch 2 and before release 3, within 6.00–6.50 s, wrist-relative angular path must be at least .350 rad and maximum orientation departure at least .250 rad, under the same retention rules. The measured most-complete partial estimates are .59–.74 rad path and .34–.49 rad supported product; the lower thresholds allow reconstruction uncertainty in this brief event.
  5. Final in-hand turn. After catch 3, within 7.40–10.10 s, wrist-relative angular path must be at least 4.00 rad and maximum departure at least π/2 rad, under the same retention rules.
  6. Finish holding. During 10.10–10.50 s, retain the ball for at least .330 s, with contact in at least 95% of samples and no gap longer than .020 s. Ball-center drift in the wrist frame is at most .012 m; mean relative translational speed is at most .060 m/s. These allow about twice the measured drift plus ball-size/contact-compliance uncertainty, and roughly three times the measured speed. Mean wrist-relative angular speed must be at most 1.50 rad/s, an explicit operational interpretation of “hold”: the partial final-hold texture diagnostic is about .4 rad over .37 s and includes noise/small real regrasping. No exact video angular speed is claimed.

The long-turn thresholds are explicit operational interpretations of TASK.md's “spin,” informed by the partial texture measurements. They do not assert a uniquely measured number of revolutions. A 4 rad path plus π/2 excursion requires a substantial turn rather than high-frequency jitter. Exact spin axis and direction are unspecified. Wrist-relative rotation is computed as R_wristᵀ R_ball; path sums shortest incremental rotation angles and excursion uses geodesic distance. Thus rotating the whole wrist alone does not earn a turn.

Wrist resemblance and hardware requirements

For every one of the 13 recorded scene-analysis phases, compare the robot root with the retargeted human wrist: orientation RMS error ≤20° and 95th-percentile error ≤30°; relative-position RMS error ≤.040 m and relative vertical-position RMS error ≤.030 m. Wrist vertical span over the episode must be .195–.295 m (measured .245 m, ±.050 m for reconstruction and actuator/contact effects). The palm normal must retain positive upward component at every step (tilt <90°), consistent with all visible phases. Use native-time interpolation, with no post-hoc time warp.

These are substantive resemblance checks, not a fixed-wrist shortcut. The reference's peak wrist speed is 9.671 rad/s while executed targets are limited to 6 rad/s. Its largest .3224 rad increment needs at least 53.73 ms versus 33.33 ms in the video. Simple rate limiting gives up to 15.77° lag (RMS 1.51°); measured reconstruction sensitivity adds up to 6.19°. The stated angular tolerances therefore permit hardware-respecting retargeting without silently claiming exact replay is feasible. Position tolerances include the .0194 m sensitivity and controller/morphology effects. Exact fingertip tracking is not required because its reconstruction uncertainty reaches .049 m; real contact and task motion are required instead.

Use the right Wuji Hand 2.2, its specified link masses/inertias and joint limits/armatures, the floating wrist impedance controller, 1 kHz finger PD and the specified effort/speed limits and encoder noise. At every physics tick, all 20 joints must satisfy lower−.02 ≤ q ≤ upper+.02, |qd| ≤ URDF speed, |tau_applied| ≤ effort. All emitted targets must be finite and correctly shaped; finger targets are clipped to limits, wrist targets respect 2.5 m/s and 6 rad/s rate/feed-forward limits and 30 N/2 N·m wrench saturation. Wrist motion itself is not a hardware violation. No state clipping or hidden object stabilization may manufacture compliance. Hardware violations count as trial failures and there must be zero such violations over the entire final verification set, including failed-task trials.

The reported policy must be a closed-loop network trained with PPO. Observations are restricted to the deployable list in LEARNING.md: noisy encoders, wrist pose/velocity, previous targets, object state, step and constant task settings. Contacts, forces, torques, physical parameters and rewards may be used for training rewards/verification but must not enter the actor. Reference/residual controllers are disclosed and their standalone performance reported. No scripted full solution, online planning, open-loop replay or imitation-only network is accepted as the final policy.

Final verification set and verdict

Require all ordered task, wrist and hardware criteria in 10/10 nominal trials (seeds 0–9, exact nominal physical parameters, independent encoder noise), and at least 80/100 randomized trials (seeds 1000–1099). Also require zero hardware violations across all 110 trials. The 80% robustness threshold is an agent-chosen reliability requirement, not measured from one demonstration.

Randomized trials use independent uniform wrist/scene translation ±.003 m and initial yaw ±2°, relative ball-center offset ±.001 m per axis, initial finger positions ±.005 rad, mass .9–1.1 × .0577 kg, radius .0315–.0335 m, sliding friction .6–1.0. Inertia scales as a thin shell with mass×radius² relative to nominal 4.1e−5 kg m². The radius interval is the stated diameter tolerance. Other ranges are explicit modest stress tests of uncertain contact/initial conditions, not video measurements. Initialization settling is physical and may not reject difficult sampled trials. Nominal hardware/objects remain exactly specified. No physical randomization parameter is given to the actor.

For each verdict record the iteration/checkpoint and criteria version, every per-trial criterion value, aggregate success counts, all hardware maxima/violations, seeds and initial conditions, policy/source hashes and limitations. Save top/left/right videos of representative successful and failed episodes where they exist, with state-based rotation diagnostics for the unmarked ball. Evidence from a subset must not be presented as completion of this final set. A passing verdict is only a claim about this simulation; it does not establish real-robot transfer or an official score.
