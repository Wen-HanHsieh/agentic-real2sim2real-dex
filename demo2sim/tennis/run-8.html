Criteria text

Agent verification criteria, version1

These are my claims about my simulation, not an official evaluation. They are declared before robot simulation or policy evaluation. Reference: recorded reconstruction version3, r0017, frozen in research/reference_v3; TASK.md remains authoritative. Success must generalize to uncertain placement and physical properties while preserving the demonstrated manipulation method.

Evidence and interpretation

The ordered goal is: settle/capture the placed ball; toss and catch; turn it with fingers; toss and catch; turn it again; toss and catch; turn it again and continue holding. There is one ball and one right hand, with no environment support during the skill. Palm-up orientation, down-up launch pulses, opening fingers in flight, yielding catches and opposed thumb/finger rolling are demonstrated. The ball is never replaced or reset within an episode.

Clean flight intervals52–65,163–177,200–215 at30Hz last .433,.467,.500s; release/capture boundaries have about1frame uncertainty. Estimated upward speeds2.35,2.50,2.41m/s give rises .280,.319,.295m. Size/focal sensitivity gives .263–.340m. Long in-hand turn intervals78–151 (2.43s) and226–306 (2.67s) have about552°/544° accepted relative angular path, with incomplete texture tracking; exact full spin count is unobservable. The short turn186–199 (.43s) has about59° accepted relative path. Contiguous accepted local turns120–133,186–196,240–245 measure89.7°,28.1°,52.2° relative to the hand. Bootstrap sampling error is small, but method/hand-fit sensitivity warrants an inferred~10° allowance. Local anatomical-hand axes are recorded in analysis/rotation_evidence.json: mostly palm-normal for the first turn, mostly finger-longitudinal for the short turn, and largely thumb-side axis for the last. The large turns visibly change axes and include regrasps, not a prescribed single-axis revolution.

The anatomical palm tilt has median~32° and approximately18–50° main range. Long-turn medians~31° and35°. Wrist dips before launches span~.10–.19m; reconstructed wrist speed max1.11m/s and acceleration max15.33m/s². Long-finger PIP/DIP flexion decreases in flight: first hold-to-flight PIP changes about17°,22°,25° for index/middle/ring. Only the last307–314 frames (.233s) visibly settle; a longer final hold is required by the instruction, not claimed observed.

Monocular absolute depth is weak (propagated sigma median112mm); conditional silhouette uncertainty median20mm. Camera/scale errors are correlated. Absolute ball attitude and offsets across texture gaps are unknown. Criteria therefore use gravity, phase-relative motion and relative hand/object rotation, not absolute video coordinates or a final marked-ball attitude.

Deployment distribution and scope of the claim

The robot starting wrist and20finger positions are chosen once and recorded with the simulation. The ball is placed roughly in the palm centre with independent horizontal displacement covering at least ±15mm along and ±12mm across the usable palm region, with a broader stress set reaching ±25mm and ±20mm. Ball orientation is arbitrary. The start distribution is not restricted to the demonstration placement. Starts are physically at rest and hand-supported, checked by the independent1second hold-policy start test. Do not discard falling placements to improve evaluation: report the tested placement support and start-check failures, and adjust the starting hand pose if needed.

Nominal ball radius33.5mm and mass57.7g are public-information priors. Main randomization covers radius32.7–34.3mm, mass56.0–59.4g, sliding friction0.45–1.1, and contact damping/stiffness around the reconstructed prior. Stress evaluation also covers radius31.5–35.5mm, mass50–65g and friction0.3–1.3. The exact distributions and contact parameter mapping must be recorded with the simulation and iteration, including the soft cover response, which the video does not measure. Every gate samples the full declared deployment distribution and fixed evaluator; report stress results separately as well as their contribution to the suite distribution. Sampled evaluation cannot prove literally every possible placement or real-world performance.

Quantities and shared rules

Use the real simulated state and actual contacts for judging, never commanded poses. Let H be the wrist frame, n=R_H*(0,1,0) the right-palm normal, f=R_H*(0,0,-1) the finger direction, and R_rel=R_H^T R_ball. Relative centre velocity removes wrist linear velocity and rotational transport. A held interval means actual hand contact with no environment contact. Opposed means held with thumb and at least one other finger touching. No reset, attachment, kinematic object force, or support outside the hand may help the policy. Any floor contact is failure. All finger position, velocity and applied-torque hardware rules must hold every physics tick; no episode with a hardware violation counts.

Episode deadline18s is an explicit robot execution allowance (~1.7× the10.5s demonstration) for adaptation, recovery and a longer final hold. It does not permit indefinitely holding on instead of completing the next stage.

All stages must preserve palm-up style: palm tilt10–65° for at least95% of manipulation time, never above80°; no wrist flip. Phase-mean anatomical wrist attitude, after a single fixed heading alignment to the chosen start, must remain within30° of the corresponding reference phase mean. This allowance combines approximately8° reconstruction uncertainty, natural adaptation and the different hand morphology; it is not to be tuned to a policy. Wrist speed must remain≤1.7m/s (1.11m/s observed, about50% allowance for reconstruction/retargeting and transients). Angular speed remains within hardware. Standard gate predicate wrist.max_rotation_deg≤65° rejects large wrist-driven turns. Object rotations must be relative to the wrist and actively driven by moving fingers.

Measure angular path as the sum of shortest frame-to-frame geodesic changes of R_rel, using a20ms sampling interval, only while held/opposed and excluding changes during free flight. To avoid counting noise, ignore changes below0.25° per20ms; also require substantial net motion within a single opposed interval. Fingers must move during rolling: passive_rel_rotation_deg is bounded as below. A sequence of wrist rotations or a loose ball slipping in a static grip is not a spin success.

Ordered stage completions

  1. settled: obtain at least .20s of continuous hand support, centre relative speed≤.08m/s and ball centre remaining inside the cupped hand; thumb/opposition may form during capture. Standard predicates held_s≥.20, touching_hand_at_end=1, environment_contact_s≤0.001. This entry prepares a genuine handoff for the first toss.
  2. catch1: exactly one completed toss/catch event after settled. Each toss must release into at least .30s without hand contact, rise .20–.40m above its own release height, have upward release speed1.9–2.9m/s, and return to hand support without floor contact. These bands surround .263–.340m sensitivity and the observed .43–.50s clean flights, with tolerance for contact boundaries and morphology. Before release the wrist makes a .05–.25m dip followed by upward motion, and at least two of the long-finger PIP/DIP chains open by mean≥.15rad relative to the prelaunch hold (the observed opening is about.3–.4rad for major PIPs). The open palm stays below the ball. A catch requires ≥.20s renewed support, relative centre speed≤.12m/s at completion and the ball within .14m of the palm centre; wrist gives downward by≥.015m around capture instead of meeting it with an upward strike. Standard predicates max_rise_m≥.20, held_s≥.20, environment_contact_s≤0.001. Own event judge checks release-relative rise and ordering; the global measurement alone does not establish a toss.
  3. spin1: while supported, finger-driven in-hand rolling achieves ≥270° cumulative relative angular path and ≥70° net change within one opposed interval. Maintain at least .30s opposed contact. This is below the incomplete552° estimate but requires several substantial turns rather than a token regrasp; gap/centre/model uncertainty prevents a precise revolution count. Axis changes/regra​​sps are allowed as observed. passive_rel_rotation_deg≤30°, with actual finger motion; wrist alone cannot satisfy this. Standard predicates opposed_s≥.30, opposed_rel_rotation_deg≥70°, passive_rel_rotation_deg≤30°, environment_contact_s≤0.001.
  4. catch2: after spin1, complete the second toss/catch, with the same event/style rules as catch1. No extra toss may substitute for an in-hand turn.
  5. spin2: after catch2, achieve ≥30° cumulative held relative path and≥20° net change within one opposed interval, with at least .10s opposed contact and passive_rel_rotation_deg≤15°. The observed local28°±~10° and full short-phase59° support a distinct smaller turn; the threshold is not borrowed from the long turn. Standard predicates opposed_s≥.10, opposed_rel_rotation_deg≥20°, passive_rel_rotation_deg≤15°, environment_contact_s≤0.001.
  6. catch3: after spin2, complete the third toss/catch under the same event/style rules. There must be exactly three substantial toss/catch events by task completion.
  7. spin3_hold: after catch3, achieve ≥270° cumulative held relative path, ≥40° net change in one opposed interval and opposed_s≥.30, with passive_rel_rotation_deg≤30°. The observed local52° and incomplete544° phase path motivate these bounds. Then hold for a full1s, with hand support on≥95% of samples, opposed contact on≥80%, relative centre speed≤.05m/s, relative angular speed≤.5rad/s and centre displacement≤.015m throughout that final second. World ball speed≤.08m/s and angular speed≤.8rad/s rule out merely carrying/spinning it indefinitely. This extends the observed .233s ending to operationally meaningful continued holding, as TASK.md requires. Standard predicates touching_hand_at_end=1, touching_environment_at_end=0, last_second_max_speed_m_s≤.08, last_second_max_angular_speed_rad_s≤.8, opposed_rel_rotation_deg≥40°, passive_rel_rotation_deg≤30°, environment_contact_s≤0.001. The event judge checks the final rolling phase and1s stability separately.

The evaluator reports the seven stages only in order, plus diagnostics for each release, catch, roll and style violation. Each primitive suite uses only the criteria of its link and actual predecessor handoff states. Skill goal-relative orientations may be chosen to realize the measured local axis/angle; final ball attitude is not itself a task goal. Primitive reorientation specs use the recorded connected reference intervals, and their mandatory gate predicates remain in force. Long-turn task path criteria are additional to the local net-angle feasibility/skill tests.

Pass rate, evidence and ambiguity

Task success means all seven stages, every shared rule and all hardware rules hold in one uninterrupted episode. Required final task success rate: ≥0.80 on128fresh gate episodes, with zero hardware-violation episodes allowed. The rate is my declared threshold for a meaningful repeated manipulation skill over uncertain starts, not an official benchmark; report its Wilson interval and per-stage funnel rather than imply certainty. Development gates use at least32fresh episodes, broadened when needed; final selection uses development results only. Each learned primitive must meet≥0.80 from its handoff distribution before whole-task PPO training. Feasibility controllers require≥0.25 but should be improved toward≥0.90 before distillation/training.

All successes/failures are supported by complete state logs, independent standard measurements, standard top/left/right videos and viewed gate montages. Final verdict must name its final gate and iteration. A controller/reference baseline is not a learned policy. The verified final policy is PPO-trained and observes only permitted state/history; it never observes contacts, forces, physical parameters, rewards or images. If a criterion proves physically incompatible with the specified hardware, record the calculation and evidence before any separately versioned revision; never lower it simply to pass a policy.

Open issues are stated rather than filled with invented evidence: exact ball revolutions, hidden fingertip contacts, absolute monocular depth, material coefficients and long-duration real-world holding are unresolved by this one video. The simulation and stress tests must address them, and its verdict will remain a simulation-only claim.
