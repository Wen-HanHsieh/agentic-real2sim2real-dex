Tennis demo-to-sim self-verification criteria v1

These are my simulation criteria, not an official evaluation. Source: TASK.md and reconstruction v1 of demo/tennis_IMG_0005_h264.mp4, 30 fps. They are written before any policy evaluation.

Ordered task

  1. Start supported in a cupped right hand. Toss and catch once (video release/capture about frames53/67).
  2. Spin the ball while supported, then toss and catch again (spin frames69–163, flight164–177).
  3. Spin again while supported, then toss and catch a third time (spin178–200, flight201–216).
  4. Spin once more, and finish holding the ball (frames217–314).

A state machine accepts exactly this sequence. Spin progress before the preceding catch cannot satisfy a later stage. Additional accidental releases during spin or final hold fail that episode. Initial preparation and time spent stabilizing may vary. Maximum episode duration20 seconds is an explicit assumption allowing slower robot execution than the10.5-second human clip.

Measured completion conditions

  • Toss: no ball–hand contact for at least0.12s continuously, upward world ball-COM excursion at least0.05m above its release height, and a descending segment before capture. At apex its bottom is at least0.01m above the highest supporting palm surface directly below it. All ball motion during flight comes from gravity and collision dynamics, without constraints, attachment, state resets or object actuation. The reference has three roughly0.45–0.50s flights and about0.23m rise. The thresholds preserve clear separation by more than one ball radius and a resolved flight of120 physics ticks; lower height than human is an explicit retargeting allowance for the1m/s wrist target speed limit. Report actual heights and airtimes, rather than claiming exact amplitude matching.
  • Catch: after descending free flight, renewed ball–hand contact on at least80% of samples for0.15s; ball-COM remains within0.16m of the palm centre; mean ball speed relative to palm below0.30m/s over that interval. The ball is65mm wide and the robot fingers extend about0.1m; the distance bound includes fingertips but excludes distant floor-supported objects. The duration is roughly five demonstration frames; speed bound tolerates rolling during the regrasp.
  • Each in-hand spin: while within0.16m of palm centre and supported on at least80% of samples, integrate ball angular velocity relative to wrist over a contiguous interval of at least0.20s. The norm of the integrated rotation vector must be at least pi/2 radians, and the net change of ball orientation relative to wrist at least pi/3 radians. Angular path alone cannot satisfy the criterion through cancelling oscillations. These are explicit minimum quarter-turn/60-degree-net assumptions: the unmarked sphere's exact angle and axis are unobservable, while TASK.md explicitly requires spin. Report both values for all three spins, contact fraction and durations. Rigidly carrying a nonrotating ball with wrist rotation does not count.
  • Final hold: after the third spin, support contact on at least90% of samples for1.0s, relative speed RMS below0.15m/s, ball-COM within0.16m of palm centre. One second is a deliberate stability test beyond the brief terminal demonstration hold.
  • No loss: no floor contact, no invalid state, and no unintended contact-free interval longer than0.08s outside a declared toss/catch transition. No ball teleportation or forces other than gravity/contact.
  • Hardware: every physics step respects all20 joint positions within URDF limits plus0.02rad allowance, absolute velocities no greater than vendor limits and applied torques no greater than vendor efforts. Commands are finite and correctly shaped. Report maximum fractions of speed/effort bounds and position exceedance. Wrist actuator uses specified gains, force/torque norm bounds and executed-target speed limits.

Demonstration similarity

Retarget the reconstructed right-hand wrist using wrist-to-middle-MCP direction and the index/little-MCP palm normal to map to robot -Z/finger direction and +Y/palm normal. One constant world translation and yaw aligns the initial pose; no per-frame alignment. Compare policies at corresponding normalized sub-event phases, permitting task timing changes. Record the transformation, interpolation and time mapping with the simulation.

Require wrist position RMSE at most0.08m and orientation geodesic RMSE at most35 degrees against that reference, and outward palm normal within45 degrees of world up for at least90% of the episode. The video is predominantly palm up;8cm allows morphology/launch scaling on a roughly20cm demonstrated wrist excursion;35 degrees allows wrist-frame retargeting uncertainty while disallowing a fundamentally different grasp orientation. Report actual errors, palm-up fraction and wrist paths. Ball geometry must remain the specified65mm sphere of mass0.0577kg and inertia about4.1e-5kg m2.

Evaluation and verdict

Run12 complete episodes with held-out seeds1000–1011 after training, with ball initial horizontal displacement independently uniform±2mm, mass multiplier uniform[0.95,1.05], sliding friction uniform[0.7,1.0], encoder noise sigma0.002rad/0.05rad/s. No episode cherry-picking. These modest ranges probe local robustness because unknown felt/rubber contacts are the main modeling uncertainty. Success requires at least10/12 episodes satisfying every criterion in order. Publish per-episode and per-stage metrics and three-view videos of a success and a failure if either exists. A passing verdict is limited to this modeled distribution, not hardware or the exact human trajectory.

Ambiguities and limitations

There is no prescribed initial pose, exact timing, spin axis or angle, toss height, or final grip. Initial conditions will follow the held/palm-up reconstruction. A sphere has no observable rotational gauge in the video; orientation in simulation supplies the measurement, not a claimed reconstruction of exact spin. Object friction/restitution/compliance are inferred and disclosed. Ground-truth simulated object pose and velocity may be observed by the policy; a real visual estimator is outside this claim. No force/tactile/current observations are supplied to the policy. Criteria revisions, if any, are separate recorded versions with reasons.
