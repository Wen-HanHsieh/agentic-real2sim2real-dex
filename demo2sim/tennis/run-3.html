Tennis self-verification criteria v1

These are my claims about my simulation, not an official evaluation. Written before policy training/evaluation. Sources: TASK.md, the 315-frame human clip, reconstruction v1 (to be recorded before simulation), and research/demo_measurements.json. The updated demo2sim/VERIFICATION.md was re-read on 2026-11-03 before writing this document.

Evidence and uncertainties

At 30 Hz, detached flight is frames 52–67, 163–178, and 200–216; nominal release times 1.733, 5.433, 6.667 s and catches 2.267, 5.967, 7.233 s. Boundary uncertainty is about two frames (0.067 s). Fitted apex rises above the first detached center are 0.288, 0.321, 0.300 m; durations including detached frames are 0.533, 0.533, 0.567 s. Raw independent acceleration estimates, before enforcing gravity, are 9.33, 9.97, 9.90 m/s². Flight fit center residual is 14–24 mm. Diameter 65 mm anchors metric scale; focal length is an inferred monocular prior, so systematic depth error remains.

The initial quiet hold occupies frames 0–38 (1.30 s), followed by a launch dip. After first catch, a prolonged finger regrasp/spin occurs at frames 81–153 (2.433 s inclusive). Between the second catch and third release, the available spin/regrasp time is frames 179–199 (0.700 s). After third catch, final regrasp/spin is frames 230–301 (2.400 s), followed by a quiet hold at 302–314 (0.433 s). All contact is right hand–ball, no support or second hand. Thumb and opposing fingers visibly exchange contact while the palm supports the ball.

From the fitted hand, the wrist's position spans 0.148 m forward, 0.050 m laterally, and 0.234 m vertically; peak wrist speed 1.139 m/s. Retargeting uses robot +Y = fitted palm normal, robot −Z = wrist-to-middle-MCP, and +X = Y cross Z. Initial wrist is translated to (0,0,0.65) m, with the same reconstruction world axes. The palm's tilt from upward has median 33.0°, 5–95% range 22.2–41.1°, full range 17.7–56.4°. The entire wrist orientation (including heading and distal direction), not only palm tilt, is the orientation reference. Three unsupported hand frames (46,47,113) are invalid in the reconstruction; only the derived retargeting reference interpolates these short gaps, explicitly as inference.

Ball orientation and exact human spin angle are unobservable for this nearly uniform sphere. I do not claim to have measured them. TASK.md explicitly requires three spins. I interpret a meaningful spin as at least 90° of net relative rotation during a contact-supported regrasp; this is an explicit task assumption, fixed before training. It is stricter than merely detecting nonzero angular velocity, but cannot establish exact imitation of human ball rotation.

Ordered episode tests

Physics runs at 1 kHz; metrics include every physics tick, with hardware and contact checks at that rate. Full skill duration is 10.5 s, matching the clip. A constant vertical translation aligns the reconstruction with the simulation; no per-episode best-fit time warping or rotation is allowed.

  1. Initial hold. For the first 1.20 s, the ball stays within 0.14 m of the wrist, has hand contact on at least 95% of ticks, and its center speed relative to the wrist is below 0.15 m/s on at least 90% of ticks. The video is quiet for 1.30 s; 0.10 s excludes two-frame boundary uncertainty and a small settle transient. The initial observed wrist-to-ball distance is about 0.11 m; 0.14 m permits robot versus human palm geometry. The speed bound allows approximately one ball radius of repositioning over 0.2 s, not a toss.
  2. Exactly three physical tosses and catches. A toss is a maximal interval with no ball–hand contact lasting at least 0.15 s; contact gaps shorter than 0.012 s are merged to avoid solver chatter (much shorter than a video frame). Each of the three counted flights must last 0.43–0.68 s, rise 0.24–0.38 m above its release center, have one upward-to-downward vertical-velocity crossing, and return to hand contact without floor contact. Compared with measured 0.533/0.533/0.567 s and 0.288/0.321/0.300 m, these bounds allow two-frame transition uncertainty plus 14–24 mm fitting error and robot finger geometry; they still require a substantial true ballistic toss. During each flight, least-squares vertical acceleration is −9.81 ± 0.5 m/s² and horizontal center drift from release is at most 0.08 m (video fitted horizontal motions are centimeters; allowance is roughly a ball diameter plus depth uncertainty).
  3. Timing and catches. The three releases are respectively within ±0.25 s of 1.733, 5.433, 6.667 s, and catches within ±0.25 s of 2.267, 5.967, 7.233 s. This tolerance includes measured 0.067 s boundary uncertainty and up to 0.18 s retargeted finger-closing delay; it preserves the long first regrasp and rapid second regrasp. After each catch, ball center stays within 0.14 m of wrist and hand contact is present on at least 90% of the next 0.15 s (about five video frames, enough to distinguish capture from a single bounce). A fourth qualifying toss fails.
  4. Three contact-supported spins in order. Between catch 1 and release 2, catch 2 and release 3, and catch 3 and the final 0.40 s hold, the ball must each achieve at least π/2 rad of net rotation relative to the wrist from its orientation at the start of that interval. Rotation is the SO(3) geodesic angle of R_wrist(t)^T R_ball(t) relative to its value at interval start, maximized over the interval; angular path length alone does not count. During the rotation interval, hand contact must be present on at least 90% of ticks and center remain within 0.14 m of wrist. At least two distinct hand links must contact the ball at some time in each spin, including one thumb link and one opposing finger link over the interval; they need not remain simultaneously contacting. This encodes visible finger exchanges, avoids counting a tossed rotating ball or mere whole-wrist rotation, and implements the explicit 90° assumption above.
  5. Final hold. In the last 0.40 s (video quiet hold 0.433 s), contact occupies at least 95% of ticks, center remains within 0.14 m of wrist, relative center speed is below 0.10 m/s for at least 95% of ticks, and ball angular speed relative to wrist is below 1.0 rad/s for at least 95% of ticks. Orientation is unobservable in the human final hold; the angular-speed threshold is an explicit physical interpretation of “hold,” allowing less than 23° drift in 0.40 s. No further toss is allowed.
  6. Demonstration wrist similarity. Over the whole clip, root-position RMS distance to the retargeted fitted wrist is at most 0.040 m; full wrist-orientation geodesic RMS is at most 20°, and its 95th percentile at most 30°. Palm normal must have upward component at least cos(65°) on at least 99% of ticks. The 4 cm position allowance is about half a robot palm width and covers 1–3 cm monocular/retargeting uncertainty. The orientation allowance covers the unobserved forearm roll and robot palm geometry while retaining the measured palm-up, approximately 33° tilt and heading. The 65° cap adds about 9° to the fitted maximum tilt of 56.4°, preventing an inverted or vertical hand from counting as similar.
  7. Physics and hardware. No ball–floor contact, object teleportation, non-contact object force, hidden grasp weld, or kinematic ball control. Every tick obeys hardware/SPECS.md: q within URDF limits ±0.02 rad, absolute joint speed at most each URDF speed, applied torque at most each URDF effort. Wrist force/torque and executed-target speed limits use the specified controller. Any violation fails the episode; demand saturation is reported separately. Actions must be finite. Simulation mesh/contact approximations and observation privileges are disclosed.

Evaluation protocol and verdict

Evaluate a fixed set of 20 full episodes, seeds 1000–1019, with ball initial offsets independently uniform ±2 mm, finger initial offsets ±0.01 rad, ball radius uniform 0.0315–0.0335 m (specified diameter tolerance), mass uniform ±5% around 0.0577 kg, and sliding contact coefficient uniform 0.7–0.9. Nominal physics is separately reported. These small perturbations test numerical repeatability and plausible object uncertainty; the 90% success requirement is my explicit robustness assumption. All seven episode tests must pass in order in at least 18/20 episodes, with no hardware violations in any of the 20. Report per-episode, per-criterion numbers, successful and failed rollout evidence where available, top/left/right videos, checkpoint and iteration ID, and wrist errors for every evaluated iteration. A subskill or curriculum success never counts as full-task success.

The PPO network must be closed loop and determine the reported actions. If a reference or controller is used under a residual, evaluate and disclose its performance alone; a base that meets these criteria makes that formulation inadmissible. No official score, real-robot transfer, or exact human spin reconstruction is claimed.
