Criteria text

Tennis skill: self-verification criteria v2

These are this experiment's own criteria, not an official evaluation. Original thresholds recorded in v1 before robot simulation or policy training; v2 adds final-hold velocity measurements only. Every threshold and evaluation rule is unchanged. Evidence: TASK.md, reconstruction v1, native 30 fps video, research/recon/analysis/measurements.json and analysis.json. All criteria below are required, in order. No threshold will be relaxed to admit a trained policy.

Understanding, observations, and TODO list

One right hand begins with the ball supported/grasped, wrist palm-up. It dips and rises to toss, opens during flight, and catches with the same hand. The fingers turn/regrasp the ball, followed by another toss/catch. A brief turn/regrasp and dip precede the third toss/catch. A longer series of finger turns ends in a hold. There is no second hand, support fixture, or floor contact in the manipulation.

  1. Hold the ball in the palm-up right hand.
  2. Toss and catch it once.
  3. Turn the ball relative to the palm, then toss and catch it again.
  4. Turn it again, then perform the third toss/catch.
  5. Turn it once more and end holding it.

Direct observations: contact/separation, projected silhouettes, palm facing up, wrist dip/rise, finger motion and visible changing seam appearance. Estimated: metric ball trajectory, wrist pose, contact boundaries. Inferred: occluded contact and calibrated scale. Unobservable in the specified uniform, unmarked target sphere: precise material orientation, spin angle and spin axis. The measured demonstration does not supply a reliable numeric rotation trace; rotation thresholds below are explicit operational interpretations of the instruction, not recovered measurements. The short interval after the second catch includes a regrasp rather than a clean isolated spin; TASK.md establishes that a spin is intended there.

Measurements and tolerances

Frames 0–33 are the initial hold. First unsupported flight: 52–66, first catch 67. Second flight: 163–178, catch 179. Third flight: 199–215, catch 216. Boundary uncertainty is approximately one frame at either end. Contact-free durations are 0.500, 0.533, 0.567 s. Reconstructed vertical rises from the first fully separated frame are 0.285, 0.323, 0.389 m; these are conservative measurements because true release occurs between frames. Independent fitted acceleration norms are 9.742, 9.753, 9.930 m/s². The calibrated focal is 1248 px, close to the learned prior of 1305 px. Metric position uncertainty for visible ball centers is about 8 mm and larger at blurred/occluded transitions.

The wrist remains palm-up: reconstructed palm-normal tilt from vertical is approximately 16° / 24° / 36° at the 10th/50th/90th percentiles (refined values in measurements.json). Wrist span is approximately 0.11 m horizontally and 0.20 m vertically. Human-to-robot wrist frames are retargeted by the middle-finger direction and thumb side; an arbitrary rigid world rotation/translation is allowed, then fixed for the episode. The reference motion may be smoothed to remove frame noise, but its times, orientation and dip/rise phases remain fixed. The ball's initial held pose and fingers may be adapted to the robot geometry.

Exact simulation checks

State and contacts are logged at 1 kHz, with policy targets at 500 Hz. A hand contact is an actual collision contact between ball and robot geometry, with normal force greater than 0.01 N (well below ball weight 0.566 N; excludes numerical grazing). A flight is a maximal interval with no hand or floor contact. Intervals under 0.08 s are not counted as tosses: the measured shortest flight is 0.50 s, and 0.08 s only filters contact chatter. A catch is renewed hand contact after a descending flight, followed by at least 0.10 s with contact present on at least 80% of samples and ball center within 0.12 m of the palm center. These tolerances cover compliant contact chatter and the robot's approximately hand-sized span.

  • Initial hold: support/contact on at least 80% of samples in the first 1.0 s. Demonstration: 1.10 s initial hold; 0.10 s margin accounts for initialization/retargeting settle. Ball stays within 0.12 m of palm.
  • Three tosses and catches: exactly three counted flights, each launched from and caught by the same right hand. Release times must be within 0.20 s of 1.733, 5.433, 6.633 s. This permits a small phase shift to accommodate robot contact mechanics, less than the shortest inter-toss preparation interval. Flight durations must be within 0.067 s of 0.500, 0.533, 0.567 s (two 30 fps boundary uncertainties). Rise above the ball center at actual release must be at least 0.260, 0.298, 0.364 m respectively (measured rises minus 25 mm, covering combined metric/transition uncertainty); no upper bound other than the duration and wrist requirements. During free flight, fitted vertical acceleration must be within 0.5 m/s² of -9.81 (about 5%; reconstruction disagreement is under 2%). Every flight must end in a catch as defined above.
  • Three in-hand spins, in order: after catch 1 and before toss 2; after catch 2 and before toss 3; after catch 3 and before the final hold. For each, accumulate rotation of the ball relative to the actual wrist using consecutive relative rotation matrices. Cumulative path angle must reach at least pi radians, and a relative orientation at least pi/2 from that phase's initial orientation must be reached. The half-turn path plus quarter-turn displacement defines a macroscopic spin and rules out tiny vibration accumulating a misleading angle. These are explicit instruction-driven assumptions because the unmarked sphere does not yield a measured angle. Hand support must be present for at least 80% of the chosen spin interval, so flight spin alone does not count. Additional spin is permitted.
  • Final hold: at least the last 0.40 s of the 10.50 s episode has hand contact on at least 90% of samples; ball within 0.12 m of palm; relative center speed RMS below 0.15 m/s and ball angular speed RMS below 2 rad/s. Demonstration: final held segment frames 301–314 spans about 0.47 s, so a 0.40 s requirement leaves two frames for the uncertain stop boundary. Measured final-hold ball-to-human-palm center speed RMS is0.0548m/s raw and0.0489m/s after Gaussian sigma1frame smoothing at30fps; relative center coordinate spans are5.7/7.7/6.5mm (research/final_hold_measurements.json). The unchanged0.15m/s threshold is about3times this reconstructed RMS, covering metric uncertainty and robot settling. Ball angular speed remains unobservable; its2rad/s threshold explicitly defines residual spin allowed during settling and is not a recovered video rotation measurement.
  • Wrist resemblance: time-aligned retargeted wrist orientation error RMS at most 20° and 95th percentile at most 35°; wrist position error RMS at most 0.06 m after the fixed initial alignment. The orientation allowance covers monocular hand-fitting uncertainty and robot anatomy while retaining the observed palm-up pose (typical tilt about 24°); position allowance covers hand-size retargeting and the measured rapid wrist transition over one video frame. Palm normal must have positive world Z for at least 99% of samples. Wrist speed limits, force norm 30 N, torque norm 2 N m, and the specified impedance controller apply.
  • Physical integrity: no floor contact; no external ball forces or constraints except gravity and collisions; no attachment/weld, teleport, reset or reference forcing during an evaluation. All finger positions within URDF limits plus 0.02 rad allowance, all speeds within URDF maxima, all applied torques within effort limits. Report saturation and any numerical limiter usage separately. Only deployable observations may be used by the policy.

Evaluation and success

Evaluate complete episodes from the held initial state, without mid-episode resets or reference-state initialization. Use fixed evaluation seeds 1000–1019, disjoint from training seeds, with independent uniform +/-2 mm initial ball position, +/-2° wrist rotation, +/-0.01 rad finger angles, and +/-0.01 m/s initial ball velocity. Initial relative orientation of the sphere may vary freely. The nominal physics are specified in the simulation record; report any training or evaluation parameter randomization. The perturbations represent small placement/encoder errors; this is a local skill test, not a claim of broad robustness.

An episode passes only if every criterion passes in order. The claimed policy passes this self-verification only if at least 18 of 20 episodes pass. Publish all per-episode metrics and representative three-view success/failure videos. Report the untrained reference/base controller separately. A verdict applies only to this simulation and these operational spin assumptions; it cannot establish real-hardware transfer or recovery of unobservable human ball rotation.

Version2 reason: supplement the quantitative evidence for the final-hold speed threshold after computing it from the recorded reconstruction. All numeric thresholds, ordering, episode perturbations and18/20 success rule remain exactly those of version1. Earlier verdicts remain against version1.
