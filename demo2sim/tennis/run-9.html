Tennis manipulation — agent's verification criteria, version 1

These are my own simulation claims, not official success criteria or a score. Written before building or evaluating the robot simulation. Sources: TASK.md and reconstruction version4 (r0012, research/recon_v5), plus native video frames. The interpretation must remain fixed during policy comparisons; changes require a new recorded version and a physical reason.

Evidence and interpretation

One right hand holds one felt-covered tennis ball above an upward-facing palm, dips and raises the wrist, opens the fingers for a free toss, receives the descending ball with a yielding wrist, and closes the fingers. The thumb and opposing long fingers roll/regrasp the ball between tosses. There are three toss/catch cycles, then a final retained turn and hold. No environmental support is used.

Deployment distribution and global conditions

The chosen fixed start pose must support placement before any policy action: one1.0s settle followed by0.5s stability hold. No start is rescued by closing the fingers after placement. Simulation must include the provided right hand's masses, limits, armature, finger PD, floating wrist impedance, rate limits, encoder noise and per-tick hardware rules.

For my evaluation, initial ball centre offsets in the palm plane cover a20mm-radius disk around the chosen nominal, with20% stress placements in the20–30mm annulus. Azimuth and ball orientation are uniform. The release can be0–8mm above the collision-free placement surface. No failed placement is silently resampled. At least95% must pass the standard start check; placement failures count as task failures. These ranges operationalize “roughly in the middle” plus occasional worse placement, not a guarantee outside them.

Evaluation varies ball diameter65.4–68.6mm and mass50–65g (ITF priors65.4–68.6mm and56.0–59.4g, widened mass for mismatch), effective ball/hand sliding friction0.4–1.2, shell inertia coefficient I/(m r²)0.5–0.70, and contact compliance/damping across plausible soft-pad to rigid-ball behavior. The recorded simulation must state exactly which ranges it realizes; unimplemented ranges remain an explicit verification limitation, never a claimed test. Physical parameters are hidden from the deployable policy.

An episode is20s at the target's500Hz interface, with1kHz hardware control. Every finger joint must satisfy hardware/SPECS.md at every controller tick. The ball must never touch the floor or any environmental support. The wrist must remain in the given workspace. A policy error, a placement failure, a floor contact or any hardware violation makes the episode fail. Sampling logs at50Hz is permitted, but hardware violations and contact events must be accumulated at1kHz so brief failures are not missed.

Ordered task stages

All stages must complete in this order. The evaluation must detect events from simulated states and contacts, not infer success from time or commanded actions.

  1. ready — From the deployment placement, retain the ball in the hand for at least0.5s, including at least0.2s of thumb-and-opposing-finger support before launch. No environment contact. This is the entry into a launch-ready grip.
  2. catch_1 — Perform one toss and capture it. A toss requires a continuous hand-free interval of at least0.35s, apex at least0.25m above the last-contact centre height, and return to hand contact with downward ball velocity before capture. A capture requires subsequent hand-only support for at least0.3s, an opposed grip for at least0.15s and relative ball speed below0.10m/s over the final0.10s of that support. Horizontal flight displacement must be at most0.15m from release. Standard max_rise_m>=0.25 is a necessary additional predicate; it alone cannot establish a toss.
  3. spin_1 — After catch1, rotate the ball at least90deg relative to the wrist within one continuous opposed interval, while fingers are actively moving; then retain it for at least0.15s. Passive relative rotation within this spin may be at most30deg. The spin must end before the second release.
  4. catch_2 — Repeat the toss/capture conditions after spin1.
  5. spin_2 — Repeat the active opposed90deg turn after catch2, before the third release. The video does not determine its exact angle; this is the explicit instruction-based assumption above.
  6. catch_3 — Repeat the toss/capture conditions after spin2.
  7. spin_hold — Perform another active opposed90deg turn after catch3, then retain the ball through the end. Over the last1.0s: hand-only support, relative centre drift<=10mm, relative linear speed<=0.05m/s, ball angular speed<=0.30rad/s, wrist speed<=0.03m/s. The1s duration operationalizes “hold” and is deliberately longer than the0.433s observed final interval; the speed thresholds define stable retention, not a measured demo angular speed.

For each spin, use standard opposed_rel_rotation_deg>=90, passive_rel_rotation_deg<=30, opposed_s>=0.3, environment_contact_s=0, and the episode's goal_rel_orientation with final goal error<=25deg. The motion's learned goal is approximately104deg about the reference's palm-normal-dominated direction, transformed relative to the current object and hand, rather than an absolute world/object orientation. The task evaluator additionally checks each spin in its own ordered phase; global maximum rotation cannot stand for three separate turns.

Toss thresholds are conservative lower bounds:0.25m versus measured0.37–0.41m permits about0.12m of release-height uncertainty from one blurred30Hz frame at roughly2.7m/s plus geometric uncertainty;0.35s versus0.47–0.50s permits about3frames of contact-boundary/retargeting difference. They still require a clearly airborne toss, not a lift or a palm bounce. The0.15m lateral limit expresses the nearly vertical demonstration with placement adaptation. Capture tolerances allow compliant settling without accepting a ball that is still escaping.

Demonstrated motion style

The task evaluator also checks the following in each corresponding phase:

  • Palm-normal tilt relative to upward: entry20–40deg; held spin phase median15–45deg; flight phase median0–45deg; final hold15–45deg. Every launch preparation must reach at least35deg tilt and never exceed75deg. These bands contain measured medians20–33deg and launch peaks41–60deg, with about10–15deg for retargeting and inferred depth/orientation.
  • Each toss includes a wrist dip followed by an upward stroke of at least0.08m, then at least0.02m of wrist descent toward capture. Upstroke threshold compares with0.111–0.197m measured, allowing roughlyone frame of wrist timing and root-frame/geometry differences. A stationary wrist while the fingers eject the ball does not meet this style.
  • During free flight, mean actual long-finger PIP flexion decreases by at least0.20rad from its pre-release held value, and increases again after capture. The measured human decrease is roughly20–30deg;0.20rad leaves about10deg for human/robot joint mapping. The rule concerns motion, not a fixed joint pose.
  • Within a spin, wrist rotation from its phase-start orientation is at most25deg. The clear reference turns5.4deg at the wrist while the object turns103.6deg; this margin permits adaptation while excluding a wrist flip as the main action.

The standard measurements enforce independent necessary predicates. The custom evaluator must check all timing, phase-local rotations and wrist/finger style above. Its boolean is allowed to be stricter than the standard predicates; it may never override a failed standard predicate.

Primitive chain and verification

The task chain is ready -> toss_1 -> spin_1 -> toss_2 -> spin_2 -> toss_3 -> spin_hold. Each toss primitive includes both release and capture, and each spin includes retention/transition into the next action. Separate feasibility suites prove each link in chain order from actual handoff states, at success>=0.25 with no hardware violations. Deployable skill suites require>=0.80 from handoff states before whole-task RL. Reused controller/network weights still need each link's handoff gate. The reference of the rolling mechanics is the clear4.000–4.467s interval; unresolved middle/final orientation is never used as precise ground truth.

A whole-task episode succeeds only if every stage and all global/style conditions pass, in order. My task pass rate is at least0.80 on the final fresh-seed gate, with zero episodes containing a hardware violation. I choose80% because a repeated dexterous sequence should succeed reliably across placement and physical uncertainty, while finite simulation does not justify a universal guarantee. Use at least32fresh episodes for dev gates and at least64for the final gate, report counts and Wilson95% intervals, and do not select checkpoints using final outcomes. These empirical limits do not establish real-world success. Target trials are judged separately against the same goal/style conditions and all must be judged successful in the latest round before stopping for success.

Limits and ambiguities

Single-camera depth, ball scale/focal coupling, contact forces, friction, mass distribution and much of sphere rotation are unresolved. The final simulation is a model, not the deployment setting. Fitted gravity is imposed in smoothed flight references; raw fits10.07/9.95/9.54m/s² provide the independent scale/time check. The calibrated rigid-floor rebound is a public prior, not a measured hand-pad restitution. A passing simulation verdict must state which deployment variations were actually tested, reference these limitations, and include target judgment/replay evidence where available.
