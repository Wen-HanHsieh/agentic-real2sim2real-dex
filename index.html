Agentic Real2Sim2Real: Closed-loop Feedback Learning

Three autonomous Codex agents each learn a dexterous manipulation skill for the 20-joint Wuji Hand 2 from a single human video. Each agent builds its own simulation, trains a policy, deploys it to the black-box SuperDex target, and improves from what comes back: only a video of the trial and the robot's own joint logs, with no scores or labels.

1 · Human videoone demonstration per task, plus a screenshot of the target
→
2 · Simulationthe agent models robot and object from public specs
→
3 · Policyits own choice of method, trained on 4 GPUs
→
4 · SuperDexone trial per submission, 100 submissions
→
5 · Feedbacktrial video + joint logs; the agent judges progress itself

Each agent works alone: separate Linux user, no access to the other agents or to target internals, same instructions for all three (see Resources).

Three tasks

Same robot and protocol; different skills. Pictures show the target environment at the start of an episode.

ENPIRE design choices: adopted, adapted, omitted
ENPIRE elementHereHow
Immutable environment and verification APIsadoptedThe verifier runs inside the cell, versioned and hash-identified; agents receive its report and cannot change how it is computed.
Multi-view vision to avoid single-view false positivesadoptedThree fixed, synchronized, calibrated RGB-D views (top, left, right) fused in 3D for every measurement.
Per-rollout records: state, action, video, resultadoptedEach submission keeps its package, robot log, three videos, calibration and sensor report, hashed in an immutable record.
Idea tree / hill-climb timelineadaptedhistory and note: append-only hypothesis → change → evidence → decision records, diagnostic vs improvement, best-so-far by sensor score. The agent still chooses its own direction.
Vision queries as function callsadaptedFrame queries after the rollout (any view, any instant, RGB + depth). Live queries would pause dynamics or change the 500 Hz policy interface, so they are not offered.
Hard limits end a rollout immediatelyadaptedTrials run to the end in physical time; one published hardware specification is enforced by SuperDex, reported by the sensor verifier and checked by a tool the agents can run on their own trajectories.
Held-out evaluation the agent cannot tune onadoptedVerifier development and acceptance used separate trial sets; the operator's ground-truth comparison stays private.
Learning algorithms, policies, planners, controllersomittedNot imported: agents keep full autonomy over methods.
Wrist cameras, real-robot resets, VLM judgesomittedThe hand is fixed and has no wrist; the cell resets itself; a deterministic geometric verifier replaces a learned judge.

Reference: ENPIRE (NVIDIA GEAR), github.com/NVlabs/ENPIRE and arXiv 2606.19980. No ENPIRE learning algorithms, policies, planners or controllers are used.

Initial research prompt (identical for all three agents)
# Autonomous Dexterous Manipulation Research

Your goal is to reproduce the dexterous manipulation skill demonstrated in the human video and develop a reliable policy for the target environment.

Read the provided task documentation and resources before starting.

## Research Autonomy

- Independently choose your simulator, algorithms, policy architecture, rewards, curriculum, and training strategy.
- Freely use available GPUs, papers, GitHub, datasets, and external tools.
- Design and iterate on your own simulation and training environment.
- Maintain a concise research log of hypotheses, experiments, results, and decisions.

## Closed-loop Learning

SuperDex is a black-box target environment. Interact with it only through the documented evaluation API.

- Develop and validate solutions locally before submitting.
- Use `r2s2r-preflight` and remote `validate` to check deployment compliance.
- Submit policies and independently evaluate task progress and failure modes from evaluation videos and robot joint logs.
- Use these findings to guide subsequent experiments and improve your approach.
- Use the **100-submission budget** efficiently.

`SUCCESS.md` defines evaluation criteria, not mandatory training rewards. You may design your own learning objectives.

## Rules

- Do not access target internals, previous research rounds, or other agents' workspaces.
- Do not modify or exploit target evaluation conditions.
- Respect all hardware, deployment, and evaluation constraints.
- Distinguish measured results from assumptions. Never claim unverified success.
- Make research decisions autonomously without waiting for human approval.

**Objective:** Autonomously discover, develop, and refine a solution that reliably reproduces the demonstrated skill in the target environment.
Evaluation protocol in brief
  • One trial per accepted submission, from a fixed initial setup; 100 submissions per agent; policy at 500 Hz.
  • Episodes: ball 10 s (5000 steps), pen 12 s (6000), cube 40 s (20000).
  • Feedback: a two-view video of the whole trial and the robot's joint logs (q, q̇, targets). No success labels, scores, object state or diagnoses.
  • Packages are checked first (local r2s2r-preflight, remote validate); rejected packages cost nothing. Policies run in an isolated sandbox: no network, no subprocesses, read-only package.
  • Progress rubrics in each SUCCESS.md are guidance for the agents' own assessment; official success is decided privately by the evaluator.
